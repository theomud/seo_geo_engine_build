What happens if your dog is taken at Dubai airport?

That fear is real — and it has happened to people. The good news: a hold is almost always caused by one fixable thing — incomplete paperwork. Here's exactly what triggers it, what it costs, and the three steps that prevent it.

Why this fear exists

A pet owner in a UAE group described it plainly: “without it your dog will be taken away in airport and never give back… still when I remember I crying.” A real account — and exactly the outcome the steps below are designed to prevent.

Quick answer

A pet is held at the UAE border when its paperwork is incomplete — most often a missing import permit or an invalid rabies record. The requirements are published by the regulator, MOCCAE, and they are specific: apply for the import permit online before travel, keep it inside its 90-day validity, and make sure the rabies vaccination is at least 21 days old.

  • Import permitApply online, before travel
  • Permit valid90 days
  • Rabies≥ 21 days before
  • Release fee if heldAED 500 / dog

Do these and a hold won't happen

The 3 steps that prevent a hold

Apply for the MOCCAE import permit online — before you fly

No pet may enter without it. It's issued by MOCCAE, the only body that issues the permit.

Time the 90-day permit window to your flight

The permit is valid for 90 days from issue. Line it up with your travel date so it's still valid on arrival.

Make sure the rabies vaccination is ≥21 days old and recorded

Microchip must be implanted before the vaccine, or the record is invalid. Bring the documented batch numbers.

On the titer-test cost — we won't guess

MOCCAE publishes no official figure for the rabies titer test. Community reports put it around AED 700–1,300 — treat that as community-sourced, not official, and confirm with the lab. We'd rather say “we don't know exactly” than invent a number.
