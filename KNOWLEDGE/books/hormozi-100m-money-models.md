# $100M Money Models — Alex Hormozi

Why it matters: The definitive practical breakdown of how to sequence offers so that a business recovers its customer-acquisition cost in 30 days or less — making growth self-funding instead of cash-constrained.

---

## Core thesis

Most businesses bleed cash because they have one offer and a slow drip of profit. Hormozi's answer is to design a deliberate sequence of four offer types — Attraction, Upsell, Downsell, Continuity — that together form a "Money Model": a system that makes more money from each customer faster than it costs to get them. The 30-day payback threshold is non-negotiable: if you can clear customer-acquisition cost within a billing cycle, you can reinvest indefinitely without needing capital. Every chapter is a proven template the author has personally used across gyms, software, services, and ecommerce doing $200M+ in annual revenue.

---

## Key frameworks

### 1. The Four-Offer Sequence (The Money Model Structure)

**What it is:** A sequential architecture — Attraction → Upsell → Downsell → Continuity — where each layer solves a different cash-flow problem.

**Structure:**
- Attraction turns strangers into customers (cheaply or freely)
- Upsell maximises revenue per customer in the first 30 days
- Downsell captures customers who said no to the upsell
- Continuity locks in recurring revenue after the initial transaction

**When to apply:** Use all four together for a full Money Model. A business with only one layer is a "front end," not a business.

**The governing rule:** Cover your customer-acquisition cost within 30 days. Credit-card float (interest-free for 30 days) becomes your growth engine if you can close that loop.

---

### 2. Attraction Offers — Five Models

Attraction Offers lower or remove the price barrier to convert strangers into first-time customers. Free and discount exist on a continuum; they are interchangeable tactically.

**2a. Win Your Money Back**
Customer pays a deposit; if they meet defined criteria (actions, results, or both), they receive the amount back as cash or store credit. The real money comes from customers who succeed and then buy more — not from those who fail. Key execution notes:
- Spread store-credit winnings over a longer-term offer rather than returning them in a lump sum.
- Build mandatory check-in meetings into the criteria — these become structured upsell moments.
- Make criteria easy to track, aligned with outcomes, and include an element that promotes the business (referrals, reviews, social posts).
- Only use this if your refund rate is below 5%; fix product quality first if it is higher.

**2b. Giveaway (Scholarship / Sweepstakes model)**
Advertise a high-value Grand Prize to gather leads. One person wins the Grand Prize; everyone else is contacted privately and offered a discounted "partial scholarship" — a promotional version of the same product. Because entrants raised their hand for the Grand Prize, they are already qualified leads. The promotional offer anchors against the Grand Prize value, making even a modest discount look substantial. Add two deadlines: one to enter, one to claim the promotional offer (seven days each). Add a second Grand Prize and award it to anyone whose referral wins — this doubles lead flow through word-of-mouth.

**2c. Decoy Offer**
Advertise a stripped-down, minimal version of your core product (the "decoy"). When leads engage, present both the decoy and a far more valuable premium option side by side. The contrast makes the premium look obviously superior. The decoy version ensures you close everyone — those who take the premium generate outsized profit; those who take the decoy become leads for future upsells. Ask "Are you here for free stuff or lasting results?" to get permission to lead with the premium version.

**2d. Buy X Get Y Free**
Bundle multiple units (or extended time) and frame the pricing so most of the value appears "free." The same total price as selling items individually, but "Buy 1 Get 2 Free" dramatically outperforms "33% off" in perceived value. Rules: give more free than paid (Buy 1 Get 2 beats Buy 2 Get 1); test mixing different free items with the paid item; raise base prices before the promotion so margins hold. For recurring businesses, "Buy 6 Months Get 12 Months Free" drives large upfront payments while keeping total revenue identical.

**2e. Pay Less Now or Pay More Later**
Give prospects a binary choice: pay full price later (with a conditional satisfaction guarantee and card on file) OR pay a discounted price now with bonuses. The "pay later" option lets you advertise as free. Once their card is on file, present the "pay now" discount — 20–50% off plus exclusive bonuses. Optimise by adjusting discount depth until the split between pay-now and pay-later matches your cash-flow needs. If more than 10% of pay-later customers cancel, your promise is too ambitious, conditions are too easy to satisfy without value, or price is too high.

### 3. Upsell Offers — Four Models

Upsells are "whatever you offer next." They are often where the majority of profit lives because current customers buy at a higher rate than strangers.

**3a. Classic Upsell ("You Can't Have X Without Y")**
Every offer solves one problem and reveals another. The Classic Upsell immediately solves the next problem. Structure: identify what problem your core product creates, then offer the solution at the moment of purchase. Cascade upsells as long as there are problems to solve. Use "You don't want anything else, do you?" framing — customers trained to say "no" will say "no" to skipping the upsell, meaning yes to buying it. BAMFAM: always Book A Meeting From A Meeting so upsell opportunities are scheduled rather than lost.

**3b. Menu Upsell (Unsell → Prescribe → A/B → Card On File)**
Four-step process for multi-product environments:
1. Unsell — cross out what the customer does not need (builds trust and focuses attention)
2. Prescribe — tell them exactly what they do need and how to use it, as if they already own it
3. A/B — ask which of two similar options they prefer (removes the yes/no decision entirely)
4. Card On File — "Want to just use the card on file?" removes friction at payment
Bonus: "The Economist Play" — if you have two options and want customers to buy both, add a third "decoy" option priced the same as the bundle. Most people then choose the bundle because the standalone options look like poor value.

**3c. Anchor Upsell (Present Premium First)**
Show the 5–10x premium version first, let the customer have "The Gasp," then ask what makes the premium version premium — and whether they care about those features. When they say they don't need the premium features, present your core offer at the lower price. Anchored customers feel relief and see the core offer as a bargain; some still buy the premium. Pricing rule: "The only thing worse than making a $1,000 offer to someone with a $100 budget is making a $100 offer to someone with a $1,000 budget."

**3d. Rollover Upsell (Credit Past Purchases Forward)**
Credit some or all of a previous purchase toward the next, more expensive offer. Use for: re-engaging old customers (winback campaigns), rescuing upset customers instead of refunding them, poaching competitors' unhappy customers, and upgrading current customers. Spread the credit over a longer period rather than giving it as a lump discount — "$600 credit applied as $50/month off a 12-month plan" keeps customers paying rather than on a free ride. Price the next offer at least 4x the credit amount so the discount stays at or below 25%.

### 4. Downsell Offers — Three Processes

A "no" to one offer is not a no to all offers. Downsells adjust how the customer pays or what they get.

**The cardinal rule:** Never lower the price of the same offer. Offer something different for less. Dropping the price of the identical product destroys trust and signals the original price was dishonest.

**4a. Payment Plan Downsell (Change How They Pay)**
Seven-step cascade, stopping at the first yes:
1. Present with interest built in; offer a "prepay discount" (same math, better frame)
2. Third-party financing → credit card → layaway
3. Half now, half on their next paycheck
4. Desire check (1–10 scale; proceed only if 8 or above)
5. Split into three payments
6. Equal payments spread over the service duration
7. Free Trial with penalty (next model)
Key finding: annual billing produces 2% monthly churn vs. 10.7% for monthly billing — always start with the longest/largest payment option and work down.

**4b. Trial With Penalty (Conditional Free Trial)**
Instead of a free trial with no stakes, give a free trial contingent on completing defined actions. If they skip the actions, they pay a penalty fee. This turns a passive free trial into an engaged onboarding sequence. The criteria mirror the Win Your Money Back criteria: attendance, homework completion, activation steps. Each criterion meeting is a structured upsell opportunity. Breaking the penalty into smaller per-infraction fees (rather than one large lump fee) generally produces better compliance.

**4c. Feature Downsell (Change What They Get)**
When a customer rates their desire below 8/10, move from payment structure to product structure. Offer fewer features, smaller quantity, older model, or a completely different product that fits their budget. This is not discounting — it is genuine personalisation. The goal is to find the highest-value thing they will actually say yes to.

### 5. Continuity Offers (Recurring Revenue)

Continuity converts one-time buyers into ongoing subscribers. Hormozi positions continuity as the final layer that transforms volatile monthly income into compounding revenue. Key principles extracted:
- Billing cadence directly controls churn: longer billing intervals (quarterly, annual) produce dramatically lower cancellation rates.
- Build continuity into the Attraction Offer from the start — the Win Your Money Back and Rollover Upsell models are explicitly designed to transition customers into ongoing subscriptions.
- Apply rollover credits across the longest period the customer will agree to so they remain active subscribers rather than one-time recipients of free service.
- The hyper-buying cycle (the window after a new commitment is made) is when continuity offers close most easily — make them immediately, not later.

---

### 6. Pricing Logic — Cross-Cutting Rules

- **30-day payback rule:** Design the offer stack so gross profit from a new customer covers acquisition cost within 30 days.
- **Price anchoring:** Always present a higher-priced option first. Anchors make lower offers feel like bargains rather than costs.
- **Reframe discounts as gains:** "It's $15, or you can prepay and save $5" outperforms "It's $10 plus $5 interest." Same math, different psychology.
- **Charge for what you give away:** Guarantees, warranties, insurance, and onboarding can each be priced rather than given free — many customers will pay a 5–50% premium for them.
- **Magnetic Middle:** Add a third pricing tier to nudge customers toward the tier you want most to sell. Price the unwanted tier irrationally to make the target tier look like an obvious choice.
- **Free beats discount in perceived value** but produces lower show-up rates. Discount leads show up more reliably — calibrate based on whether no-shows are costly.

## Big takeaways

- A Money Model is a deliberate sequence of offers, not a single price point.
- The rental car desk and the burger combo are the canonical examples of fully-deployed Money Models — every moment of purchase reveals the next solvable problem.
- Upsells typically generate more profit than the original sale; a business with one offer has only a front end.
- Never lower the price of the same thing — change the offer or change the payment structure instead.
- Billing frequency is a lever on lifetime value: annual billing customers cancel at roughly one-fifth the rate of monthly billing customers.
- The hyper-buying cycle (window of new enthusiasm after a commitment) is the optimal time to make every upsell and continuity offer.
- Rollover credits are more powerful than discounts because they feel like earned rewards and tie the customer to the next purchase.
- Refund criteria, qualifying actions, and trial penalties all function as structured engagement sequences — design them to deliver results, not just reduce refunds.
- "Everyone thinks businesses make money on people who fail the program. No. The real money comes from people who succeed with it and you have something else to offer them."

## How we use it in the engine

1. **Offer stack first, content second.** Before writing any service or landing page, map the four-layer sequence for that offer. Content should support conversion at each layer, not exist independently of offer logic.
2. **Price anchor on every pricing page.** Lead with a premium tier (5–10x core price) before presenting the core offer. Use the Magnetic Middle for three-tier pages.
3. **Every lead magnet is an Attraction Offer.** Apply the Win Your Money Back or Decoy Offer structure to all lead-gen assets — define the "win" criteria and the upsell at the moment of completion.
4. **Rollover credits as re-engagement mechanism.** For any winback or re-engagement email sequence, apply a rollover credit to past purchase history rather than a flat discount.
5. **Build billing cadence into continuity.** Default to quarterly or annual billing options; use the annual billing churn data (2% vs 10.7%) as the justification for recommending annual plans in client-facing copy.
