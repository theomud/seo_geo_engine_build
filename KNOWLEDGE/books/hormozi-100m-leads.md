# $100M Leads — Alex Hormozi

The definitive practitioner playbook for demand generation: how to make strangers aware of your offer and convert that awareness into a stream of engaged, ready-to-buy leads across every channel.

---

## Core thesis

Most businesses do not fail because their product is bad — they fail because not enough people know the product exists. Advertising is the process of making your offer known. Hormozi argues there are only four fundamental ways to do this (the "Core Four"), and that the real goal of advertising is not raw contacts but *engaged leads* — people who actively signal interest. The book is a systematic guide to running all four methods with skill and volume, layered with lead magnets that lower the barrier to first contact, and a content philosophy that builds trust before any sale is attempted.

---

## Key frameworks

---

### 1. The Lead vs. Engaged Lead Distinction

**What it is:** A lead is simply anyone you can contact — a phone number, email, social follower. A lead alone is nearly worthless. An *engaged lead* is a person who has demonstrated interest in what you sell: they opted in, followed you, replied, booked a call, or consumed your content. Hormozi treats engaged leads as the true output of all advertising activity, not raw contact counts.

**Structure:**
- Lead = contactable person (necessary but not sufficient)
- Engaged lead = contactable person who has shown interest (the actual goal)

**How to apply:** Measure advertising success by engaged lead volume, not impressions or follower counts. Every system you build should be designed to convert raw contacts into people who raise their hand.

---

### 2. The Core Four

**What it is:** A 2×2 matrix derived from two variables — *audience temperature* (warm = people who know you; cold = strangers) and *communication scale* (one-to-one = private; one-to-many = public). Combining these yields the only four methods by which any person can make any other person aware of anything.

**Structure:**

| | People who know you (warm) | Strangers (cold) |
|---|---|---|
| **One-to-one (private)** | #1 Warm Outreach | #3 Cold Outreach |
| **One-to-many (public)** | #2 Post Free Content | #4 Run Paid Ads |

**Each method in brief:**

**#1 Warm Outreach** — Direct, personal contact with people already in your network (calls, texts, DMs, email). The cheapest method, highly reliable, and the recommended starting point for any new product or business. Uses a 10-step process: compile a contact list from every platform you have ever used, personalize each message with a genuine opener, reach out to ~100 people per day, use the ACA conversational framework (Acknowledge, Compliment, Ask) to warm the exchange, then surface an offer framed around the value equation (dream outcome, likelihood of success, speed, ease). Hormozi's first clients came entirely from this method.

**#2 Post Free Content** — Public, one-to-many communication to a warm audience (social media, email newsletters, podcasts, YouTube). Every content unit has three jobs: hook attention, retain it, and reward it. The goal is growing a warm audience that trusts you before you ever ask them for money. Content should always be written *for strangers*, not insiders, so it can spread. Monetization comes through either integrated CTAs (short asks embedded in long-form value) or intermittent asks (occasional promotional posts amid a stream of pure value). Hormozi argues free content also quietly amplifies every other channel — prospects who consumed his content before a sales call closed at dramatically higher rates.

**#3 Cold Outreach** — Direct, personal contact with strangers who have not given you permission (cold email, cold DM, cold calling, cold LinkedIn). Sits atop warm outreach as the more advanced cousin — same mechanics, different audience. Requires research and personalization at the opening so the message does not read as spam. Volume matters: reaching hundreds or thousands of targeted contacts per day through automated sequences is the lever.

**#4 Run Paid Ads** — Public, one-to-many broadcast to cold audiences through paid platforms (Meta, Google, YouTube, TikTok, etc.). The only method with no natural ceiling on scale, but it costs money and requires the ability to measure return. The creative unit (ad) must hook, retain, and reward just like organic content, then direct the viewer to either a lead magnet or a core offer page.

**When to apply:** Hormozi recommends starting with warm outreach (#1) to validate an offer cheaply. Add free content (#2) in parallel to build a growing warm asset. Layer in cold outreach (#3) when warm contacts are exhausted. Use paid ads (#4) once unit economics are understood. All four compound when run simultaneously.

### 3. Lead Magnets

**What it is:** A lead magnet is a complete solution to a *narrow* problem — typically free or very low cost — that attracts interested strangers, delivers genuine value, and naturally reveals the larger problem your core paid offer solves. Think of it as a free "first hit" that earns trust and appetite for more.

**Structure — Seven Steps to an Effective Lead Magnet:**

1. **Define the problem and audience.** Pick a narrow, specific pain that sits one step upstream of your core offer's problem. The magnet solves step one; your offer solves the rest.
2. **Choose the solution type.** Three options: (a) reveal a problem the prospect did not know they had; (b) give a sample or trial of the core solution; (c) deliver one step in a multi-step process, with the remaining steps requiring your paid offer.
3. **Choose the delivery mechanism.** Four formats: software/tool, information (course, guide, checklist), service (done-for-you), or physical product. Each format can apply to any of the three solution types — that yields up to twelve possible lead magnet variants for a single problem.
4. **Test the name.** The headline drives 80% of the decision to engage. Test multiple titles and subheadlines against your audience before committing. Small word changes (adding "how to," swapping "advertising" for "leads") can multiply response rates.
5. **Make it easy to consume.** Offer multiple formats (video, audio, text, PDF). Remove every barrier — fewer steps, fewer clicks, faster delivery.
6. **Make it genuinely good.** Treat the magnet as if you could charge for it. If the prospect thinks the free thing is worth their time, they assume the paid thing is worth their money.
7. **Make the next step obvious.** The thank-you page or fulfillment email should surface the core offer and make requesting it frictionless — a clear, single call to action with a reason to act now (scarcity, urgency, social proof).

**Key design principle:** The magnet must solve one narrow problem *and* make a second, larger problem visible — the one your core offer addresses. The salty pretzel gets you thirsty; the drink costs money.

---

### 4. "Give Until They Ask" Content Principle

**What it is:** A philosophy for monetizing a content audience that inverts the conventional "give, give, give, ask" cadence. Hormozi's refinement: give so consistently and generously that your audience *asks you* for a paid relationship rather than waiting for you to pitch them.

**Structure:**
- Mature platforms (TV, Facebook) run roughly a 3.5:1 give-to-ask ratio — the minimum sustainable floor.
- Growing platforms give far more and ask far less, which is why their audiences compound.
- Hormozi's principle: publish high-value content without making asks until audience members initiate the commercial conversation themselves — they DM, email, or visit your site asking how to work with you.
- Mechanism: giving in public, asking in private. The public feed is pure value; the private channel (DM, email reply, booking page) is where transactions happen.
- This self-selection produces better customers — people already convinced of your value who feel they are getting the better end of the deal.

**Practical guard:** The moment you start asking publicly is the moment audience growth slows. Every commercial ask trades future compounding for present revenue. Be patient with asks; be aggressive with value.

## Big takeaways

- Engaged leads — not raw contacts — are the real metric. Redesign every funnel around that signal.
- There are only four advertising methods. If lead volume is too low, the diagnosis is either insufficient skill or insufficient volume in one or more of the Core Four.
- Lead magnets work because they solve a real narrow problem *and* reveal the next one. Weak magnets try to tease value without delivering it.
- Naming and packaging a lead magnet matters more than its contents. Test the headline before building anything else.
- Free content compounds silently. Prospects who consume content before a sales call convert at much higher rates — content is not a vanity project.
- "Give until they ask" produces the highest-quality customers and the fastest audience growth. Patience with monetization is a competitive advantage.
- The Core Four are not in competition — they reinforce each other. Cold audiences warmed by organic content convert better on ads and outreach.

## How we use it in the engine

1. **Lead magnet before every core offer.** Any landing page or service page in the engine should be preceded by a free entry point that solves one narrow upstream problem. Design the magnet using the seven-step framework; test the name before building the page.
2. **Engaged lead as the KPI.** Track opt-ins, replies, bookings, and DMs — not impressions or follower counts. All reporting in the engine should surface engaged lead volume as the primary demand metric.
3. **Core Four audit.** When a client's lead volume is insufficient, run a Core Four diagnostic: which of the four methods are they running, at what skill level, and at what volume? The gap is the prescription.
4. **Content strategy defaults to "give until they ask."** All organic content produced by the engine (blog, social, email) should be written for strangers, deliver standalone value, and carry no commercial ask unless the give:ask ratio is clearly healthy (above 3:1 in content units).
5. **Warm outreach as the validation layer.** Before investing in paid ads or cold outreach infrastructure, use warm outreach to verify that the offer and lead magnet convert. Only scale channels once the unit economics are proven cheaply.
