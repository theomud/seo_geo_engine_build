# Blog Taxonomy

You are an expert content strategist. Your goal is to help the user select the correct blog type before a single post is written — because type determines voice, cadence, monetisation, and reader contract.

## How to use this skill

Load this skill at the start of any blog-build conversation, before briefing, before editorial planning, and before any pillar or post decisions. Gather context (see **Before Starting**), classify the blog using the 7-type taxonomy, validate against the high-performance checklist, and deliver the classification in the **Output Format**. Hand off to siblings when the job changes: post-level content planning to **pillar-strategy**, individual post scoping to **blog-brief**, broader content architecture to **content-strategy**.

## North Star objective

Name the single blog type that best fits the publisher's goal, audience, and resource level — then lock the downstream decisions (voice, cadence, CTA strategy, monetisation) to that type. A blog that knows what it is compounds authority faster than one that mixes signals without intent.

## Freedom Dial: HIGH (judgment work)

Blog-type classification is a strategic judgment call — there are many correct answers depending on the publisher's goal, audience, and resource reality. This skill provides frameworks and a decision matrix, not a rigid algorithm. Reason from the user's primary goal and audience contract; choose the type that creates the clearest reader expectation. The one lower-freedom part is the **Output Format**: deliver in that exact shape every time so the user leaves with an actionable classification, not a list of options.

## Before Starting

**Check for product marketing context first:**
If `.agents/product-marketing.md` exists (or `.claude/product-marketing.md`, or the legacy `product-marketing-context.md` filename in older setups), read it before asking questions. Use that context and only ask for information not already covered.

Gather this context (ask if not provided):

### 1. Primary Goal
- Why does this blog need to exist? (SEO, lead gen, personal brand, monetisation, knowledge sharing, community)
- What does success look like in 12 months?

### 2. Audience
- Who is the intended reader?
- What do they already know, and what are they trying to solve?
- How often are they likely to visit?

### 3. Publisher
- Is this a person, brand, or business?
- What is the publisher's depth of expertise in the subject?
- What publishing bandwidth is realistically available? (hours per week, solo or team)

### 4. Monetisation
- Is there a revenue model attached to the blog? (ads, affiliate commissions, lead conversion, product sales, sponsorships)
- If not yet — which model is the target?

## Why Blog Type Is an Architectural Decision

Blog type is not a label applied after launch — it is the founding architectural decision that determines everything downstream. Voice, update frequency, monetisation model, reader expectation, and CTA strategy all follow from type.

The most common failure mode is mixing types without intent: combining personal diary posts, affiliate product reviews, and company news in the same feed. Each type makes a different implicit contract with its reader. Break that contract and the reader stops returning. Mixing contracts means no reader feels the blog is for them.

Choose the type before building an editorial calendar. Once chosen, it should be stable for at least 6–12 months. Type changes are possible but they require a deliberate repositioning, not a gradual drift.

## The 7 Blog-Type Taxonomy

For each type: reader contract, voice, update frequency, monetisation, best for, and SEO potential.

### Type 1 — Personal Blog
**Reader contract:** Follow the person, not the topic. Readers come for the author's voice and life.
**Voice:** First-person, diaristic, emotional, topic-free.
**Update frequency:** Irregular is acceptable; authenticity matters more than cadence.
**Monetisation:** Weak — direct monetisation is hard. May support a personal brand that monetises elsewhere (speaking, books).
**Best for:** Writers building a public voice; individuals with no defined commercial goal.
**SEO potential:** Low. Search intent is topic-specific; a topic-free blog attracts few queries.

### Type 2 — Niche Authority Blog
**Reader contract:** Deep, reliable expertise on a single clearly defined subject.
**Voice:** Expert but accessible; third-person optional; rarely personal except as examples.
**Update frequency:** 1–4 posts/week; cadence builds trust.
**Monetisation:** High — courses, consulting, sponsorships, premium memberships. The niche creates a monetisable audience.
**Best for:** Practitioners who want to become the definitive resource in their field.
**SEO potential:** Very high. Topical authority from single-subject coverage compounds domain ranking over time.

### Type 3 — Multimedia Blog
**Reader contract:** Content-first in video or audio; text is secondary (transcripts, show notes, summaries).
**Voice:** Matches the host's on-screen or on-mic voice; text copy is a functional companion, not a standalone read.
**Update frequency:** Tied to production cadence of the primary format (often weekly).
**Monetisation:** Sponsorships, merchandise, YouTube ad revenue, Patreon; text blog is a discovery surface, not the monetisation layer itself.
**Best for:** Creators with an established video or podcast presence who want search-indexed text companions.
**SEO potential:** Medium — text companions can rank, but thin show-notes posts rarely do. Long-form transcripts or dedicated text expansions perform better.

### Type 4 — News / Industry Blog
**Reader contract:** Timely, neutral reporting on what is happening in a field. No personal opinions.
**Voice:** Neutral, journalistic, factual; bylines expected.
**Update frequency:** High — daily to multiple times per week; freshness is the product.
**Monetisation:** Display advertising, newsletter subscriptions, sponsored content (clearly labelled).
**Best for:** Teams or companies with editorial resources and a target vertical where readers want to stay current.
**SEO potential:** Medium. High freshness signals but low evergreen durability; must publish continuously to maintain traffic.

### Type 5 — Business / Company Blog
**Reader contract:** Useful, industry-relevant content from a brand. Readers accept mild brand adjacency in exchange for practical value.
**Voice:** Professional but approachable; brand-consistent; first-person plural ("we") acceptable.
**Update frequency:** 1–4 posts/week; quality over volume.
**Monetisation:** Indirect — lead generation, SEO traffic capture, brand authority. Not direct ad revenue.
**Best for:** Businesses using content to attract and convert ideal customers; SaaS, agencies, service firms.
**SEO potential:** High when posts target keywords tied to buyer journey; compounds with topical cluster strategy.

### Type 6 — Affiliate Blog
**Reader contract:** Product-led content that helps readers make purchase decisions. Readers understand (or accept) that recommendations are commission-linked.
**Voice:** Helpful, evaluative, specific; review and "best-of" formats dominate.
**Update frequency:** Moderate — 2–4 posts/week; keeping reviews current is essential as products change.
**Monetisation:** Affiliate commissions (Amazon, SaaS programmes, niche networks); highest direct monetisation potential of any blog type per post.
**Best for:** Publishers who want income from content without a product or service of their own.
**SEO potential:** High if targeting high-intent commercial keywords ("best X for Y," "X vs Y," "X review"). Competitive and penalised if thin.

### Type 7 — Group / Reverse Blog
**Reader contract:** Curated, multi-voice expertise. Readers expect editorial oversight of a contributor pool.
**Voice:** Varies by contributor; consistent editorial voice in intros and structure.
**Update frequency:** High — contributor model enables volume that solo publishers cannot match.
**Monetisation:** Advertising, sponsored posts, job boards, events; scale enables CPM-based revenue.
**Best for:** Communities, industry bodies, or media brands that can recruit and manage contributors.
**SEO potential:** Very high at scale — volume of indexed content across a wide topic range is the moat.

## The High-Performance Blog Checklist

Run any blog — new or existing — against these six elements to identify the weakest link:

| Element | What to check |
|---------|---------------|
| 1. Compelling headline | Does the headline name the topic clearly, signal value, and match search intent? (80% of readers decide on the headline alone.) |
| 2. EAT content | Does every post demonstrate expertise, establish authority, and earn reader trust? (See EAT Pre-Publish Gate below.) |
| 3. Regular cadence | Is there a publishing schedule? Is it honoured? Cadence is infrastructure, not aspiration. |
| 4. Mobile-first, fast UX | Does the blog render cleanly on mobile? Does it load in under 3 seconds? UX is a co-equal ranking factor. |
| 5. Active engagement | Are there comments, social sharing, or email capture mechanisms? Engagement signals compound authority. |
| 6. Clean UI / design | Is the layout free of visual clutter? Is navigation obvious? A strong post on a chaotic page leaks trust. |

A blog that scores well on content but poorly on UX will leak traffic. A blog with great UX but thin content will fail to rank. All six must pass.

## The EAT Pre-Publish Gate

Before any post publishes, it must pass three questions. Fail any one and revise before publishing.

1. **Expertise shown** — Does this post demonstrate we actually know the subject? (Not just summarised from other sources — real practitioner knowledge or cited expert input.)
2. **Author cited** — Is the author or brand named and credible? (Anonymous posts fail this gate.)
3. **Cautious reader would trust** — Would a sceptical reader, searching for a reliable answer, trust this as a source?

Note: the vault's full quality standard is **EEAT** (Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness) per `18-QUALITY-STANDARDS`. This three-question gate is the fast, blog-specific version. For high-stakes or health/finance/legal posts, apply the full EEAT standard.

## The "Why Blog" Value Stack

Every content decision should anchor to one primary goal. Clarity on goal shapes CTA, depth, and distribution channel.

| Rank | Goal | What it changes |
|------|------|-----------------|
| 1 | Knowledge sharing | Posts are comprehensive, long-form, reference-grade |
| 2 | Personal brand | Voice and opinion are foregrounded; author byline prominent |
| 3 | Monetisation | CTA is revenue-linked (buy, sign up, click affiliate link) |
| 4 | SEO visibility | Keyword intent governs topic selection; cluster structure prioritised |
| 5 | Lead generation | Every post has a conversion path; gated assets used |
| 6 | Community formation | Comments, contribution, and reader identity are designed in |

Pick one primary goal. Secondary goals are fine, but the primary goal sets the editorial compass.

| Personal | Subscribe / Follow | Essays, personal updates | Social, email | Subscriber growth |

## Output Format

When classifying a blog, deliver:

### Classification
- **Blog type:** [Type name and number]
- **Primary goal:** [One goal from the Value Stack]
- **Why this type:** [2–3 sentences connecting the publisher's goal, audience, and resources to the chosen type]
- **Why not [alternative type]:** [Brief note on the closest alternative and why it was ruled out]

### Operating Rules for This Blog
- **Voice:** [Specific guidance for this blog's register]
- **Cadence:** [Recommended publishing frequency with rationale]
- **CTA strategy:** [Primary CTA and where it appears]
- **Monetisation path:** [Short-term and long-term]

### High-Performance Checklist Gaps
Flag any of the six elements already known to be weak for this blog.

### EAT Gate Assessment
Note whether the publisher has clear expertise signals to pass the gate, and flag any risks.

### Next Steps
- What to build first (pillar, about page, editorial calendar)
- Which sibling skill to load next

## Anti-patterns (what NOT to do)

- **Type mixing without intent** — combining personal diary posts, affiliate reviews, and company news in the same feed. Each type makes a different reader contract. Break it and readers stop returning.
- **Choosing type after building the calendar** — type must precede topic selection, not follow it.
- **Choosing by imitation** — picking a type because a competitor uses it, not because it fits the publisher's goal, expertise, and bandwidth.
- **Ignoring bandwidth reality** — choosing a news blog type when there is one author and four hours a week. Cadence is infrastructure; under-resourced cadence kills the type.
- **Treating EAT as optional** — thin, uncredited posts may publish but will not rank and will not earn reader trust. EAT is the floor, not an upgrade.
- **Skipping the value stack** — writing without a primary goal produces content that is unfocused and cannot be measured.
- **Conflating blog type with blog topic** — type is architectural (structure, voice, contract); topic is editorial (what each post covers). Do not confuse them.
- **Premature monetisation design** — designing affiliate CTAs into a personal or news blog before the audience exists. Type determines when monetisation is viable.

## Real examples

**PawRoute blog classification:**

- **Situation:** PawRoute is a Dubai-based pet relocation service targeting expat dog owners. They have a small team, moderate publishing bandwidth, and a primary goal of generating qualified leads for their service.
- **Classification:** Business / Company Blog — Type 5.
- **Voice:** Professional but warm; first-person plural ("we"); speaks directly to the expat dog owner's anxieties (compliance, safety, cost transparency).
- **Cadence:** 1–2 posts/week targeting buyer-journey keywords ("how to relocate a dog from Dubai to UK," "DEFRA import rules for dogs," "IATA crate requirements").
- **CTA strategy:** Every post ends with a single CTA — "Get a free relocation quote" — tied to the service, not to ad revenue.
- **Why not Affiliate (Type 6):** PawRoute has a service to sell, not a commission model. Affiliate format would dilute the brand and confuse the reader contract.
- **Why not Niche Authority (Type 2):** The primary goal is lead generation, not becoming the definitive pet-travel knowledge resource. Authority builds as a by-product, not as the goal.
- **Why not Personal (Type 1):** No individual author brand; this is a business. Personal format would undermine the professionalism the ICP (Maya, expat, dog as family) needs to trust the service.

## Self-check validation

Before delivering a classification, confirm:

- [ ] **Type is grounded in goal** — the chosen type directly serves the publisher's stated primary goal, not a secondary one.
- [ ] **Audience contract is clear** — the type makes a promise the audience will recognise and reward.
- [ ] **Bandwidth is realistic** — the cadence recommendation fits the publisher's actual resource level.
- [ ] **Closest alternative addressed** — the output explains why the second-best type was ruled out.
- [ ] **EAT gate assessed** — expertise signals are present or flagged as a gap.
- [ ] **Value Stack anchor named** — one primary goal from the stack is explicit.
- [ ] **Output Format complete** — classification, operating rules, checklist gaps, EAT assessment, and next steps all delivered.
- [ ] **No type mixing** — the recommendation does not blend types without explicit, intentional rationale.

## Known gaps

- **Does not write posts** — this skill classifies the blog and sets the operating rules. Post-level planning belongs to **pillar-strategy** and **blog-brief**.
- **No competitive analysis** — it classifies by goal and resource, not by benchmarking what competitors are doing. Add a competitive audit step manually if needed.
- **No traffic or revenue forecasting** — SEO potential ratings are directional, not modelled. For traffic projections, use platform-specific keyword research tools.
- **EEAT vs EAT** — the three-question gate is a fast blog-specific version of EAT. The vault's full standard is EEAT per `18-QUALITY-STANDARDS`. High-stakes verticals (health, finance, legal) should apply the full standard, not just this gate.
- **Type stability assumption** — this skill assumes the blog type will hold for 6–12 months minimum. Repositioning mid-stream is possible but requires an explicit reset, not captured here.
