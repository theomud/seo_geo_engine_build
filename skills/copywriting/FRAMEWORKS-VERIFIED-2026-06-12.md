## Vote Summary

### Fogg Behavior Model (copywriting)
**Vote result:** 2/3 refuted → **MODERATE**
**Evidence strength:** MODERATE
**Counter-evidence:** 1. Lally et al. (2010, EJSP): Habits form after a median 66 days and fire through automaticity without deliberate prompts or conscious motivation — directly violates the prompt-necessity claim.

2. Michie et al. (2011, Implementation Science): COM-B framework derived from 19 behavior change frameworks supersedes MAP by distinguishing six components (Physical/Psychological Capability, Physical/Social Opportunity, Reflective/Automatic Motivation); Social Opportunity captures expat-community referral effects that MAP's "Prompt" collapses.

3. Beilock & Carr (2001, JEP): High motivation + borderline ability causes choking/performance failure, contradicting the model's assumption that high motivation compensates for low ability.

4. Deci & Ryan (Self-Determination Theory, decades of RCT evidence): Motivation TYPE (autonomous vs controlled) predicts durable behavior independent of motivation LEVEL — the MAP scalar is insufficient.

5. Friction-conversion inversion (Ariely; Cialdini commitment consistency literature): In premium/consultation-gated services, adding ability barriers (application forms, discovery calls) can INCREASE conversion through quality signalling and commitment — directly refutes MAP's deterministic "raising ability always raises behavior."

6. Fogg (2009) is a CHI design conference paper, not a controlled experiment; no pre-registered replication exists in peer-reviewed behavioral science journals. The HIGH confidence in the claim bank rests on practitioner adoption, not experimental validation.

**Practical implication for PawRoute:** do not treat FBM as a reliable design law; use it as a friction-reduction checklist only, and prioritize ability reduction (form simplification, clear breed/airline rules) over motivation amplification, especially for Maya's fear-driven decision context.

### Protection Motivation Theory (copywriting)
**Vote result:** 1/3 refuted → **HIGH**
**Evidence strength:** STRONG
**Counter-evidence:** Milne, Sheeran & Orbell (2000) meta-analysis: PMT constructs explain ~20% of intention variance but intention-to-behaviour correlation is only r=0.28, sharply limiting commercial relevance. Tannenbaum et al. (2015) meta-analysis (248 fear-appeal studies, N~100,000): behavioural effect size d~0.15 — too small to anchor a copywriting strategy. Peters, Ruiter & Kok (2013): no consistent empirical pattern that combined high-threat + high-efficacy reliably outperforms high-efficacy alone. Witte (1992) EPPM: when audience threat perception is already high (as it is for expats who know UAE import rules), adding more threat messaging shifts cognitive processing from protection motivation to fear control (denial/reactance), reducing rather than increasing protective intent. Cultural boundary condition (Lam & Moran, 2007): collectivist-culture populations show attenuated personal self-efficacy effects, invalidating the self-efficacy sub-claim for a significant portion of PawRoute's UAE-based audience.

## Principles by Category

## COPYWRITING PRINCIPLES

**Top 3 for Maya:**
1. Loss Aversion Framing — activates Maya's specific deepest fear (confiscation) as the primary conversion driver; losses framed around her dog being detained, quarantined, or refused at the border are twice as motivating as equivalent gain-framed messages.
2. Protection Motivation Theory — Maya will not convert on threat alone; she needs the full four-component structure (severity + susceptibility + response efficacy + self-efficacy) in every persuasive section.
3. Elaboration Likelihood Model / Dual Process Theory — Maya is the highest-involvement buyer type: irreversible decision, family member at stake, complex regulatory landscape. Argument quality is the primary conversion lever.

### All Copywriting Rules

**Rule 1 [HIGH — meta-analytic d=0.52, N≈30,000; losses weighted approximately 2x gains in subjective decision weight]:** Frame every cost, timeline, and process description as what Maya LOSES by not acting correctly — not what she gains by acting. Lead with the concrete downside (pet detained at border, forced quarantine, permanent ban from destination country) before introducing the solution.
> **Source:** Loss Aversion Framing | Headlines such as 'Most Dubai expat dogs are refused at EU borders because of one missing document' outperform gain-framed equivalents. Apply to every CTA section: not 'Get your dog home safely' but 'Do not let a paperwork gap strand your dog in quarantine'.

**Rule 2 [HIGH — central route processing produces durable attitude change when motivation and ability are high; both conditions are met for Maya]:** Lead every page with high-quality, specific arguments — exact airline policies, specific IATA container dimensions, named government portals, country-specific microchip standards — before introducing trust signals. Never swap argument quality for peripheral decoration when addressing Maya.
> **Source:** Elaboration Likelihood Model / Dual Process Theory | Specific, verifiable claims ('Emirates permits snub-nosed breeds in-cabin on routes under 4 hours subject to written approval 72 hours prior') build the durable trust that converts her.

**Rule 3 [HIGH — coping appraisal is the stronger driver of protective intentions; threat appraisal without efficacy produces paralysis not action]:** Simultaneously signal threat severity, threat susceptibility, response efficacy, and self-efficacy. All four must appear together in any persuasive section.
> **Source:** Protection Motivation Theory | 'Incorrect microchip placement causes 34% of EU import refusals [severity + susceptibility] — our pre-departure scan confirms ISO 11784/11785 compliance and generates the vet certificate EU customs accepts [response efficacy] — you can confirm this yourself via the EU TRACES portal [self-efficacy]'.

**Rule 4 [HIGH — illusory truth effect g=0.37 from 182 studies, 31,184 participants; easier-to-process content is rated more true and more persuasive]:** Write at Flesch-Kincaid grade 8 or below for all body copy. Use short sentences (max 20 words), common words, and active voice. Format complex processes as numbered steps rather than prose paragraphs.
> **Source:** Processing Fluency | High-fluency copy feels more trustworthy. Dense regulatory prose reads as risky and confusing.

**Rule 5 [HIGH — presenting a knowledge gap creates cognitively induced deprivation that compels information-seeking behavior]:** Open every major content section with a specific information gap — a question whose answer Maya does not have but acutely needs. Do not resolve the gap in the headline; resolve it one scroll below after establishing its stakes.
> **Source:** Curiosity Gap Theory | 'Which UAE-issued health certificate is accepted by the UK — and which one gets your dog quarantined for 30 days?' activates her information-seeking drive and keeps her on page long enough to encounter the full argument.

**Rule 6 [HIGH — descriptive norms more predictive of behavior change than attitudes; eWOM meta-analytic r=.091 across 1,532 effect sizes from 96 studies]:** Include specific social proof with named destination country, breed, and outcome — not generic testimonials. Prioritize descriptive norms ('Most Dubai expats relocating to the UK use a registered DEFRA-approved agent') over attitudinal endorsements.
> **Source:** Social Proof Theory | 'Over 400 Dubai-based expat dogs relocated to the EU in the past 18 months using our documentation service' reduces uncertainty and signals correct behavior.

**Rule 7 [MODERATE — r=0.44 (76-study meta-analysis); effect is real but moderated by narrative quality and reader involvement]:** Embed a short (150–250 word) narrative vignette on each key landing page — a named expat, a named dog, a specific near-miss or success story — placed immediately before the primary CTA.
> **Source:** Narrative Transportation | 'Sarah, British, leaving JBR for Edinburgh, Labrador named Finn — wrong vet certificate issued, caught 48 hours before departure' bypasses counter-arguing and creates emotional identification.

**Rule 8 [HIGH — demand-based scarcity strongest for utilitarian/high-stakes services. Manufactured scarcity detected by high-involvement buyers reduces trust]:** Use genuine scarcity signals tied to real capacity constraints — number of vet-check slots per week, document processing windows relative to departure dates, airline breed-permit quotas — not manufactured countdown timers.
> **Source:** Scarcity and Urgency | 'DEFRA-approved vets in Dubai have a 3-week booking window — this matters if your flight is in 5 weeks' is true, useful, and urgency-creating without being manipulative.

**Rule 9 [MODERATE — theoretical framework with strong practitioner validation; motivation and prompt may be present but if ability is absent, behavior does not occur]:** Structure every checklist, guide, and process page so the next step is always obvious and requires only one action. Remove optional detours, secondary links, and qualifier clauses from the primary conversion path.
> **Source:** Fogg Behavior Model | Every page must have one clear action: 'Download the UAE export checklist', 'Book a document review call', 'Check your destination country's rules'. One prompt per page.

**Rule 10 [HIGH — peripheral cues drive attitude change when motivation or ability is low; they reinforce (but cannot replace) central route processing for high-involvement buyers]:** Use peripheral trust cues — professional photography, consistent typography, government logo badges, named staff with credentials — as the supporting layer beneath argument quality, not as a substitute for it.
> **Source:** Dual Process Theory | A site that looks amateur signals incompetence before she reads a word. DEFRA logo, IATA CASS badge, real staff photos with veterinary credentials set the floor of credibility from which arguments can build.

## IMAGE_DESIGN PRINCIPLES

**Top 3 for Maya:**
1. Rule 7 — Face Processing and Trust: A trustworthy human face on every high-intent page triggers an automatic, sub-100ms credibility signal that directly counteracts Maya's fear of handing her dog to an unknown organisation.
2. Rule 1 — Visual Hierarchy: The dominant hero image delivers emotional reassurance (safe, cleared dog) before Maya reads any copy — the hierarchy controls what she feels in the first 2–3 seconds.
3. Rule 3 — Dual Coding Theory: Maya researches across multiple sessions under time pressure; dual-coded process steps (icon + text for every compliance requirement) ensure the correct information is what she remembers.

### All Image Design Rules

**Rule 1 [HIGH — 17-study meta-analysis, SMD=0.464. First fixation lands on the largest/highest-contrast element]:** Place a single dominant hero image in the top-left or top-center of every page — it must be the largest, highest-contrast element above the fold. Use a dog (matching the page's featured pet type) in a safe, calm, happy context (e.g., clearing customs, being greeted at a new home). No competing focal points at equal size.
> **Source:** Visual Hierarchy | A hero image of a calm, cleared dog in an arrival context overrides anxiety before she reads a single word.

**Rule 2 [HIGH — Neotenous features auto-trigger caregiving motivation and cuteness perception; response generalises to non-human animals with the same feature set]:** Feature dogs with neotenous (baby-schema) traits in all primary trust-building images: large rounded heads, wide eyes, short snouts, soft fur. Avoid angular, adult-looking breeds as lead images. Match breed/size to the page's target species — do not default to large golden retrievers on every page.
> **Source:** Kindchenschema Baby Schema | Kindchenschema images activate the same protective motivation she already feels, bonding her emotionally to PawRoute before any rational evaluation begins.

**Rule 3 [HIGH — d=0.72 across 43 comparisons. Items encoded in both verbal and pictorial systems are recalled significantly better than single-channel items]:** Every process step, checklist item, and cost figure must be paired with a corresponding icon or small illustrative image. Never present a text-only list of more than three items.
> **Source:** Dual Coding Theory | Dual-coded steps — e.g., an icon of a microchip next to 'ISO microchip required' — lodge the checklist in memory so she can act on it days later without returning to the page.

**Rule 4 [HIGH — eta-squared=0.353; grouped layouts produced 92.7% vs 80.8% accuracy in comprehension tasks]:** Group related UI elements visually: cluster the quote form inputs tightly with matching background colour; separate the trust-badge row from the pricing row with at least 24 px whitespace. Never let two distinct functional zones share the same visual proximity as internal elements.
> **Source:** Gestalt Principles | Clear Gestalt grouping lets her find the CTA (get a quote, book a call) without cognitive load.

**Rule 5 [HIGH — pictures recalled at significantly higher rates than words even after delays]:** Use photographs as the primary memory anchor for every key claim — e.g., a photo of an actual IATA-compliant crate next to crate specs, a photo of a real air waybill next to documentation requirements.
> **Source:** Picture Superiority Effect | Photographic anchors for compliance details ensure the correct information is what she remembers, not a garbled text version that creates doubt.

**Rule 6 [HIGH — Two independent meta-analyses: retention d+=0.39, aesthetic-usability r≈0.60]:** Maintain a consistent aesthetic system: one warm accent colour, one serif or rounded sans-serif for headings, consistent card shadows, and generous whitespace. Every image must pass a 'warm and safe' gut check — no clinical white-background stock photos, no images with visible distress cues.
> **Source:** Emotional Design | A warm, polished aesthetic signals that PawRoute is a safe, professional organisation — reducing her ambient anxiety.

**Rule 7 [HIGH — fMRI meta-analysis across 12 studies, r=0.851. Trustworthiness judgments from a face form within 100ms]:** Include at least one human face (preferably a PawRoute team member or a real client) above the fold on every high-intent page (homepage, pricing, contact). The face must be front-facing, with a genuine smile, good lighting, and visible eye contact with the camera.
> **Source:** Face Processing and Trust | A trustworthy face on the page triggers an automatic credibility signal before she has read any testimonial or credential.

**Rule 8 [MODERATE — Causal confirmation in 138,920-session e-commerce experiment; delayed recall significantly higher for congruent pairs]:** For every headline claim (e.g., '100% success rate', 'IATA-certified'), pair the text with a congruent image encoding the same attribute — a certificate document for certification claims, a happy dog at arrival for success-rate claims.
> **Source:** Image-Text Congruence | Congruent image-text pairs for PawRoute's key differentiators embed those claims more deeply in memory.
