# Blog cheat sheet — types, strategy, and how to write lead-gen blogs

*High-level, one-page. Distils the KNOWLEDGE-BASE, BLOG-PLAYBOOK, Human Writing System & 15 Principles.*

## What a blog actually is (2026)
Not "articles." A blog is a **machine whose job sets its shape.** In 2026 the winning form is a
**structured answer engine** — content built to be found by Google *and* AI engines, to build trust,
and to convert. The objective decides everything:
- **Ad/affiliate blog** → monetises attention → optimise **pageviews/RPM**.
- **Lead-gen / service blog** (ours) → monetises **qualified enquiries** → optimise **leads & cost-per-lead, NOT pageviews.** *A post with 400 reads + 12 quotes beats one with 50,000 reads + 0.*

## The 5 blog types (pick by the reader's job)
| Type | Job | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **Educational** | answer a question (how-to, guide) | SEO/GEO, top of funnel |
| **Comparison** | help a buyer decide (X vs Y, cost) | high buyer-intent, MOFU |
| **Case study** | show proof (situation→process→result) | conversion |
| **Authority** | original frameworks/opinion | trust + brand |
| **Research/data** | original data, surveys, benchmarks | **links + AI citations** |

## Strategy (the system, not random posts)
- **Question-first:** don't ask "what should I write?" — ask "**what customer question do I answer?**"
- **Pillar → cluster:** one deep pillar per topic + supporting posts that **interlink** and point to the **money page**. Never a dead-end read.
- **Funnel mapping:** tag every post **TOFU** (informational) → **MOFU** (commercial/comparison) → **BOFU** (transactional), and link it to the page it feeds.
- **10 great > 500 thin.** Trust beats volume. AI penalises generic mass content.
- **GEO ≠ SEO:** also write to be **cited by AI** — answer-first chunks, stats, official citations, FAQ schema (citation is decoupled from ranking).

## How to write a LEAD-GEN blog (the recipe)
**The formula:** `Question → Answer → Evidence → Explanation → Story → FAQ → Action.`

**The 2026 anatomy (top to bottom):**
1. **Headline** — promise a result/curiosity ("The one form that delays most pet moves").
2. **Answer-first** (first 100 words) — the liftable answer, with a number. Don't make them wait.
3. **Key takeaways** box — many readers stop here; that's fine.
4. **TOC** + scannable **H2/H3** (people scan ~20–28% of words).
5. A **visual** — data table, flow, or chart.
6. **Original insight or a story** (Problem→Mistake→Discovery→Result→Lesson) — AI can't fake experience.
7. **FAQ** (with schema) — answers the fan-out questions; AI loves it.
8. **Official sources** cited inline — trust + GEO.
9. **One help-first CTA** → the money page / a lead magnet (checklist/calculator). Not five competing CTAs.

**The non-negotiables:**
- **Fear-first:** name the real fear, then resolve it. Enter the conversation already in their head.
- **Write to one person**, in their words ("you" > "we"); benefit/outcome before feature.
- **Official sources only; never guess.** Cite or hedge. Separate **fact** ("DEFRA requires X") from **advice** ("we recommend X").
- **Sell the outcome, not the service** (the relief, the result).
- **Capture the lead:** a low-friction form/quote + a help-first lead magnet for the 95% not ready yet → nurture.

## Lead-gen do / don't
**Do:** map every post to a stage + money page · single primary CTA · trust signals (reviews, sources, real numbers) · measure leads/CPL.
**Don't:** dead-end posts · feature dumps · vague superlatives · multiple competing CTAs · chase traffic that never converts · fabricate (people/reviews/facts).

## The 10-second test (before publishing anything)
> "If I were the perfect customer, would I feel understood in the first 10 seconds?" If no, rewrite.
The best copy doesn't sound like marketing — it sounds like someone describing your situation, then helping.
