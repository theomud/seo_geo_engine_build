## Framework 1: Fogg Behavior Model (B=MAP)

**Category:** copywriting / persuasion
**Evidence strength:** MODERATE (founding paper is conference paper, not peer-reviewed journal)
**Grade (pending full 10-vote):** HIGH
**Adversarial votes:** 0/0 run

**Core claim:** "Behavior (B) occurs if and only if Motivation, Ability, and a Prompt converge at the same moment (B=MAP); if any one of the three is absent or below threshold, the target behavior will not happen regardless of how strong the remaining factors are."

**Mechanism:** Multiplicative convergence of three psychological levers: Motivation (pleasure/pain, hope/fear, social acceptance/rejection drives the "why"), Ability (task simplicity reduces cognitive and physical friction, determining "can I do this now"), and a Prompt (an environmental cue or trigger that fires the behavior at the right moment). The model draws on established learning theory, self-efficacy theory (Bandura), and expectancy-value motivation; reducing ability friction is posited to be more reliably actionable than increasing motivation, because motivation is volatile while simplicity is designable.

**Effect size:** Cohen's d = 0.59–1.07 for behavioral outcomes (scoping review, Duarte-Anselmi et al. 2025, 6 studies); adjusted odds ratios of 8–35x higher behavior rates in high motivation+ability segments vs. low segments (Agha 2022, cross-population survey).

**Primary source:** Fogg (2009). "A behavior model for persuasive design." DOI: 10.1145/1541948.1541999 (conference paper — NOT peer-reviewed journal)

**Supporting sources:**
- Duarte-Anselmi et al. (2025). Scoping review of Fogg Behavior Model in behavior change interventions. BMC Public Health. DOI: 10.1186/s12889-025-24525-y
- Agha (2022). Use of a practitioner-friendly behavior model to identify factors. Vaccines 10(8):1261. DOI: 10.3390/vaccines10081261

**Pet relocation application:** PawRoute copy should reduce perceived ability barriers first (state exact document checklist, time estimate, step count) before amplifying motivation (pet safety, family reunion), and place a single unmissable CTA at the moment the reader has absorbed both — because all three MAP elements must converge simultaneously.

**Practical rules:**
1. Lower friction before amplifying fear: Lead with a clear 3-step process summary before describing import-refusal horror stories — motivation alone without perceived ability produces anxiety, not action.
2. Match the prompt to the moment of peak MAP convergence: Place the primary CTA immediately after the sentence that resolves the reader's biggest doubt (cost, pet safety, timeline).
3. Segment copy by motivation type: Fixed-departure-date owners → hope/deadline prompts; early research owners → social-acceptance framing.

**Limitations:** Founding paper is a conference paper (practitioner-grade at origin); 2025 scoping review included only 6 studies (median n=46); no RCT evidence in copywriting or e-commerce contexts — all evidence from health behavior interventions; model criticized as oversimplification vs COM-B or Behaviour Change Wheel; motivational effects are volatile and context-dependent.

## Framework 2: Protection Motivation Theory (PMT)

**Category:** copywriting / persuasion
**Evidence strength:** STRONG
**Grade (pending full 10-vote):** HIGH
**Adversarial votes:** 0/0 run

**Core claim:** "When messaging simultaneously elevates perceived threat severity and susceptibility (threat appraisal) AND demonstrates high response efficacy and self-efficacy for a recommended protective action (coping appraisal), it produces significantly stronger intentions to adopt protective behavior than either component alone — with coping appraisal being the stronger driver of behavioral intent."

**Mechanism:** Fear-based message triggers two parallel cognitive appraisal processes: (1) threat appraisal (severity + personal probability of harm) and (2) coping appraisal (response effectiveness + personal achievability). When coping appraisal outweighs threat appraisal, protection motivation forms. When threat dominates without sufficient coping appraisal, fear-control responses occur (denial, avoidance, fatalism) — suppressing action.

**Effect size:** d = +0.52 (moderate), from Floyd et al. 2000 meta-analysis across 65 studies, ~30,000 participants.

**Primary source:** Rogers (1975). "A Protection Motivation Theory of Fear Appeals and Attitude Change." DOI: 10.1080/00223980.1975.9915803

**Supporting sources:**
- Floyd, Prentice-Dunn & Rogers (2000). Meta-Analysis of PMT. Journal of Applied Social Psychology 30(2):407-429. DOI: 10.1111/j.1559-1816.2000.tb02323.x (65 studies, ~30,000 participants)
- Milne, Sheeran & Orbell (2000). Prediction and Intervention. Journal of Applied Social Psychology 30(1):106-143. DOI: 10.1111/j.1559-1816.2000.tb02308.x

**Pet relocation application:** PawRoute content should pair concrete threat messaging ("pets without approved documentation are confiscated at Dubai airport") with high-efficacy coping copy ("our certified route navigator shows exactly which vaccinations, microchip standard, and titre test timeline are required for your dog's specific breed and destination").

**Practical rules:**
1. Lead with credible, specific threat — name the exact consequence (confiscation, mandatory quarantine) and make it personally probable. Vague threats trigger denial, not action.
2. Immediately follow every threat statement with a high-efficacy coping statement — the response must feel both effective AND personally achievable.
3. Eliminate response-cost barriers in the same message block — address price anxiety, perceived complexity, and time cost directly alongside efficacy claims.

**Limitations:** PMT reliably predicts behavioral intentions but intention-behavior gap remains; performs less well when recommended action is complex or costly; COVID-era replications (2020-2022) confirmed that high threat without achievable coping backfired, increasing avoidance.

## Framework 3: Narrative Transportation

**Category:** copywriting / persuasion
**Evidence strength:** STRONG
**Grade (pending full 10-vote):** HIGH
**Adversarial votes:** 0/0 run

**Core claim:** "When readers become mentally 'transported' into a narrative — losing awareness of surroundings and merging cognitively and emotionally with the story world — they reduce counter-arguing and adopt story-embedded beliefs, producing greater attitude and behavior change than equivalent expository persuasion."

**Mechanism:** Transportation suppresses analytical resistance: absorbed readers suspend disbelief, experience narrative-consistent emotions, and identify with characters, all of which bypass the deliberate counter-arguing that blocks persuasion in non-narrative contexts. Operates through reduced cognitive elaboration combined with heightened emotional engagement and mental simulation.

**Effect size:** Meta-analytic synthesis of 132 effect sizes from 76 articles (Van Laer et al., 2014) — transportation is a robust antecedent and mediator of persuasion; Green & Brock (2000) original experiments: r = 0.30–0.50 between transportation scores and story-consistent beliefs.

**Primary source:** Green & Brock (2000). "The role of transportation in the persuasiveness of public narratives." DOI: 10.1037/0022-3514.79.5.701

**Supporting sources:**
- Van Laer, De Ruyter, Visconti & Wetzels (2014). Extended Transportation-Imagery Model Meta-Analysis. Journal of Consumer Research, 40(5), 797-817. DOI: 10.1086/673383
- Moriuchi & Gonzalez (2026). Narrative Transportation in Storytelling as Health Communication. Health Communication. DOI: 10.1080/10410236.2026.2634455

**Pet relocation application:** PawRoute copy should open every page with a specific owner-pet story ("On her last morning in JBR, Lena watched Mochi sniff the sea air for the final time") before introducing logistics — transported readers adopt story-consistent beliefs (safe relocation is achievable) with far less resistance.

**Practical rules:**
1. Lead with a named character and a concrete sensory moment before any mention of permits, costs, or timelines.
2. Embed the key compliance claim inside the story rather than in a separate warning box — transported readers process story-embedded facts with lower counter-arguing.
3. Match the protagonist's profile to Maya: expat female, medium-large dog, Dubai departure.

**Limitations:** Effects weaker for high-involvement audiences already motivated to process analytically; diminishes when narrative is perceived as unrealistic or protagonist is dissimilar to reader; harder to induce in short-form content; Chen & Bell (2022) meta-analysis found only small effects (d ≈ 0.10) for formal features like point-of-view manipulation — full transportation state, not just narrative framing, is needed.
