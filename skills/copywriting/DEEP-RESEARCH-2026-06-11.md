## SECTION A: WRITING STYLES TAXONOMY

### What the research tells us about style selection

**CONFIRMED ✓ T1** — Statistical/data-led style outperforms narrative on beliefs and attitudes (r = .101, small but reliable). Source: Allen & Preiss 1997 meta-analysis, Tandfonline.

**CONFIRMED ✓ T1** — Narrative persuasion has only a SMALL overall effect (r = .063, N = 9,330). It is validated, not dominant. Deploy selectively. Source: Zebregs et al 2015 meta-analysis, Tandfonline.

**CONFIRMED ✓ T1** — Processing fluency is the master lever: easier-to-process content is judged as MORE TRUE across all style types (conceptual, perceptual, linguistic fluency). Source: Reber, Schwarz & Winkielman 2004, PSPB.

**KILLED ✗** — The "power-matching" hypothesis (low-power audiences prefer warm tone, high-power prefer competence) did not survive verification. Do not apply this rule. Source: Dubois et al 2016 — verifiers found the claim overstated.

**KILLED ✗** — "Print/text narrative had no significant effect in meta-analysis" — killed. This was a reading of one moderator analysis; the primary finding (r = .063 small effect) stands.

**Practical rule:** Every style benefits from fluency (clear sentence structure, plain words, short paragraphs). The styles differ in *emotional register and persuasive mechanism*, not in whether fluency matters — fluency matters in all of them.

### STYLE 1: Plain-Language Instructional

**Psychological mechanism:** Processing fluency → perceived truth and trustworthiness. Easier to read = more credible. (CONFIRMED ✓) When information is complex and the reader is anxious (Maya at midnight), clarity itself is reassurance. Reduces cognitive load, preserves working memory for decision-making.

**When to use:** Middle funnel. FAQ pages, route guides, documentation checklists, breed-restriction pages.

**Who it works on:** Persona 4 (Confused Researcher), Persona 3 (Last-Minute Mover), any high-anxiety first-timer.

**Research:** Alter & Oppenheimer 2006 (fluency); NN/G legibility/readability/comprehension hierarchy (⚠️PENDING re-verify, but CONSENSUS-level standing).

**How to write it:** Short sentences. Subject-verb-object. No jargon without definition. One instruction per sentence. Numbered sequences over prose paragraphs. Active voice always.

**20 examples adapted to pet relocation:**

1. Your dog needs a microchip before anything else. No microchip means no airline acceptance, anywhere.
2. The export permit takes 3–5 working days. Apply before you book your flight date.
3. IATA regulations require the crate to be at least 10cm taller than your dog when standing. Measure twice.
4. Dubai MOCCAE issues the health certificate. Your vet cannot issue it — only MOCCAE can.
5. You need six documents total. We obtain five of them for you.
6. The UK's PETS Travel Scheme requires a rabies antibody blood test. Results take 30 days minimum.
7. Most pet owners need 10–12 weeks from first contact to flight day. Start the process now.
8. South Africa requires an import permit before your dog departs Dubai. Without it, your dog will not clear customs.
9. We verify your exact route against current IATA regulations. Rules change faster than websites update.
10. Snub-nosed breeds (Bulldogs, Pugs, French Bulldogs) are banned from cargo on most airlines. We find the exceptions.
11. Your health certificate expires 10 days after issue. Time the vet appointment precisely.
12. The sequence is: Microchip → Health Certificate → Export Permit → Airline Approval. Every time, in that order.
13. The blood titre test must be done at an OIE-approved laboratory. Most Dubai vet labs do not qualify.
14. IATA crate requirements are not guidelines. Airlines check at check-in. A wrong crate means your dog stays behind.
15. You do not need a pet passport in the UAE. You need an export health certificate from MOCCAE.
16. Import rules at destination change without notice. We verify current requirements within 72 hours of your booking.
17. Some routes require a fit-to-fly certificate within 48 hours of departure. We will tell you if yours does.
18. The UAE export fee is AED 200 for the first pet, AED 100 for each additional one.
19. Three documents are required at Dubai airport check-in: health certificate, microchip record, IATA-compliant crate label.
20. We have done this exact route before. Here is what you need, and in what order.

### STYLE 2: Data-Led Analytical

**Psychological mechanism:** Statistical evidence has the strongest impact on beliefs and attitudes (r = .101 confirmed). Numbers create a sense of objective reality, reduce subjective doubt, and activate the brain's truth-assessment via processing fluency (precise numbers process more fluently as authoritative than vague claims). [EB: Cialdini authority principle amplifies this — credentialed source + data = highest belief-change lever.]

**When to use:** Mid-to-late funnel. Comparison pages, cost guides, route fact-sheets, "Why choose us" sections, safety pages.

**Who it works on:** Persona 4 (Confused Researcher), Persona 5 (Safety-First), corporate HR buyers, analytical personality types.

**How to write it:** Use specific numbers (not "many" — "47 routes"). Cite the source in-text (NN/G-style inline reference). Avoid rounded numbers where exact figures are available — "32 breeds" not "around 30."

**20 examples adapted to pet relocation:**

1. Emirates accepts 32 dog breeds in cargo. 14 are restricted. We have mapped all 32.

5. Average pet relocation cost on the Dubai→UK route: AED 8,000–16,000 depending on breed, route, and airline. We provide exact figures before you commit.
6. IATA Live Animal Regulations are updated annually. The 2025 edition contains 23 changes affecting crate specifications.
7. Dogs weighing over 32kg travel cargo on most airlines. Only 4 airlines on Dubai routes accept large-breed pets in cabin.
8. The UK's quarantine requirement for compliant pets was abolished in 2012. Your dog will not go to quarantine if documentation is correct.
9. South Africa's import permit processing time averages 21 working days. We submit applications on Day 1 of engagement.
10. We have completed relocations on 47 routes from Dubai. The 3 highest-documentation routes are UK, Australia, and New Zealand.
11. Microchip scanner failure happens in approximately 1 in 200 dogs. We test microchip readability at every vet visit.
12. 94% of our clients receive an initial WhatsApp response within 2 hours of first contact.
13. Emirates SkyCargo IATA acceptance rate for pets we have booked: 100% on first submission.
14. UAE health certificates are valid for 10 days from issue. We schedule vet visits to ensure maximum buffer before departure.
15. The most common cause of pet delays at Dubai airport: non-compliant crate dimensions (accounting for 47% of delays we have investigated).
16. We track regulatory changes on 12 destination countries monthly. Last update: 3 June 2026 (South Africa import permit requirements).
17. Average client self-reported stress score (1–10): 8.2 at first contact, 3.4 on departure day. We track this.
18. Documentation errors account for 89% of pet travel failures. Experience reduces this to near zero.
19. The UAE–Australia route has an 8-step documentation sequence and a 3-week minimum lead time.
20. We have never had a dog refused at the destination border. Documentation is why.

### STYLE 3: Narrative/Storytelling

**Psychological mechanism:** Narrative persuasion is real but modest (r = .063, CONFIRMED ✓). It works primarily through narrative transportation (the reader mentally inhabits the story) which lowers counter-arguing. Best for emotional engagement and brand memorability, NOT as a primary belief-change lever. Pair with statistics — the combination outperforms either alone. The oxytocin "trust molecule" angle for storytelling is ⚠️CONTESTED — Nave, Camerer & McCullough 2015 found cumulative evidence for oxytocin→trust does not hold up (CONFIRMED ✓ as a refutation). Do not cite Zak's narrative-trust claims.

**When to use:** Top of funnel (awareness content), testimonial/case-study pages, social media, email sequences, "About us" page. Opens a blog post, sets emotional context, then hands off to data-led or instructional style.

**Who it works on:** Persona 1 (Expat Leaving Dubai), Persona 2 (Family with Children), Persona 7 (Remote Relocator). Less effective with Persona 4 (Confused Researcher) who wants facts.

**How to write it:** Short paragraphs. Past tense for resolution, present tense for tension. Specific, named details (a 5-year-old Labrador — not "a dog"). No adjective overload. Let the situation carry the emotion.

**20 examples adapted to pet relocation:**

1. Sarah had booked her flights. She had found a flat in Edinburgh. The one thing she had not figured out was Milo.
2. The call came at 4pm on a Thursday. Job transfer. Start date: six weeks away. She had a 5-year-old Labrador and no plan.
3. Three websites told her three different things. One said quarantine. One said no quarantine. One gave her a number that rang out.
4. He had been with her through two apartments, one divorce, and a promotion. He was not staying behind.
5. The morning her dog cleared customs at Heathrow, she cried in the arrivals hall. Not from sadness.
6. We have moved 400 dogs out of Dubai. Every single one arrived.
7. Marcus thought the whole thing would be a nightmare. He called us on a Tuesday. His Golden Retriever landed in Toronto on the Friday.
8. She typed "pet relocation Dubai" at midnight. Every website looked the same. Ours was the only one that told her what could actually go wrong.
9. The last thing Emma expected when she left Dubai was that her dog would be the easiest part of the move.
10. He was 11 years old, slightly arthritic, and nervous around strangers. We found an airline that would take him in the cabin.
11. The first thing Nadia said when she called: "I just need someone to tell me it's going to be okay." We did. Then we proved it.
12. Four years in Dubai. A cat, a dog, and a one-year-old. She had 8 weeks. We handled the pets.
13. He had never flown before. She had never relocated a dog before. Between them, they figured it out — with our help.
14. The vet who had seen her dog for four years printed the wrong certificate. We caught it 36 hours before the flight.
15. She asked us: what is the worst that can happen? We told her. Then we told her why none of it would happen to her.
16. It took us 14 hours to get her dog out of South African customs. Nobody else stayed on the phone that long.
17. The hardest part of leaving Dubai was not the flat, the job, or the timezone. It was the dog. We made that part easy.
18. He sent us a photo from Gatwick. His Border Collie, asleep on the arrivals hall floor. "She is fine," he wrote. "You are legends."

### STYLE 4: Warm-Reassuring

**Psychological mechanism:** Fear-acknowledgement + high-efficacy solution = strongest trust mechanism [EB: Tannenbaum 2015 meta-analysis, 127 studies, n=27,372, T1]. The warm register reduces psychological reactance (people stop arguing back when they feel understood). Processing fluency effect: calm, warm language is easier to process and therefore judged as more credible (CONFIRMED ✓). Unique to high-fear niches: the *tone* itself is a trust signal before the content has even been absorbed.

**When to use:** Fear-resolution pages, homepage hero, FAQ pages, first-contact touchpoints, any page where Maya is in high-anxiety mode (which is most of them).

**Who it works on:** Maya (Persona 1) primarily. Any first-time caller. Persona 5 (Safety-First). Most effective at top and mid funnel.

**How to write it:** Validate the emotion first ("It makes sense that you're worried"). Then reframe with competence ("Here is exactly what happens"). Short sentences. Avoid exclamation marks. Avoid "Don't worry" — it invalidates rather than validates. Second person always.

**20 examples adapted to pet relocation:**

1. It makes sense that you are worried. Most people calling us have never done this before. That is exactly why we exist.
2. Your dog will not be handled by strangers who do not care. We choose handlers we have met personally.
3. The documents feel overwhelming from the outside. From the inside, there are exactly six, and we get five of them for you.
4. You are not overreacting. Some routes genuinely are complicated. We will tell you honestly which ones, and why.
5. We understand why you have been avoiding this conversation. Most of our clients put it off for weeks. It does not have to be as hard as it looks.
6. No, your dog will not be confiscated at the airport — as long as the paperwork is correct. Our job is making sure it is.
7. Some people have told us they were scared to call because they thought we would make them feel foolish. We will not.
8. You have been Googling for days. The information online is confusing because the rules change per route, per airline, per breed. We cut through all of that.
9. We will tell you what is needed, when to book the vet, and exactly what to take to the airport. You will feel prepared, not panicked.
10. If something goes wrong — a paperwork delay, an airline change — we stay on the phone with you until it is fixed.
11. Your dog will travel in a climate-controlled hold, monitored at every connection. We only book routes we would put our own pets on.
12. We have done this route before. We know the customs checklist. We know what causes delays and how to avoid them.
13. The first call is just a conversation. No cost, no commitment. We just want to understand your situation.
14. Every question you have, we have heard before. There are no foolish questions when it comes to your dog.
15. We know you have read about things going wrong. They do go wrong — when people use the wrong service or try to do it alone. That is not going to be you.
16. We handle the government paperwork so you do not have to navigate Arabic-language government portals at midnight.
17. The crate requirements sound complicated, but we will send you a sizing guide this afternoon. Most pets fit in a standard size.
18. If your breed is restricted on certain airlines, we know which carriers make exceptions and how to book them correctly.
19. After working with us, our clients consistently say they felt completely calm on departure day. That is the goal.
20. We have moved hundreds of dogs. Not one has been refused at the border. Not one.

### STYLE 5: Authoritative-Clinical

**Psychological mechanism:** Authority principle [EB: Cialdini, T1]. Precise regulatory language signals expertise through demonstrated knowledge — the reader cannot fake knowing that "MOCCAE issues export health certificates under Federal Law No. 18 of 1993." This style also benefits from processing fluency's truth effect: precise, specific claims process as more credible than vague ones (CONFIRMED ✓). Risk: cold language activates psychological distance in fearful readers — pair with a warm-reassuring sentence at the end of each regulatory block.

**When to use:** Regulatory/compliance pages, breed-restriction guides, country requirements, airline policies. Mid-to-late funnel. After trust is established via warm-reassuring, anchor with clinical precision.

**Who it works on:** Persona 4 (Confused Researcher), Persona 5 (Safety-First), corporate HR buyers, relocation managers.

**How to write it:** Cite the law/regulation number. Use exact terminology. Short, declarative sentences. No hedging ("may", "could") where the rule is firm. Cite the issuing authority.

**20 examples adapted to pet relocation:**

1. UAE Federal Law No. 18 of 1993 governs animal exportation. MOCCAE enforces export health certification requirements.
2. All dogs entering the United Kingdom must comply with the PETS Travel Scheme (SI 2011/2883). Non-compliant animals are returned to origin at owner's expense.
3. IATA Live Animal Regulations (LAR), Edition 2025, Section 6.3 specifies minimum internal crate dimensions. Airlines do not accept substitutions.
4. Breed-specific legislation applies in: UK (Dangerous Dogs Act 1991), Australia, New Zealand, Canada, and Germany. Each publishes its own restricted breeds list.
5. The rabies antibody titre test requirement is ≥0.5 IU/ml. Tests must be conducted at an OIE-approved laboratory. UAE-based non-OIE labs are not accepted.
6. MOCCAE issues export health certificates only after physical examination by a licensed government veterinarian. Self-certified documents are rejected.
7. Emirates SkyCargo operates under IATA Resolution 620. Acceptance of live animals requires advance booking under the airline's live animal programme, minimum 48 hours prior.
8. South Africa's DAFF Import Permit for pets is issued under the Animal Diseases Act 35 of 1984. Processing time: minimum 21 working days.
9. Australian DAWE requires a minimum 10-day post-arrival quarantine at the Mickleham facility for all dogs from most countries. No exceptions are made.
10. The UK's Animal Health Certificate (AHC) must be issued within 10 days of departure and signed by an Official Veterinarian (OV). No other veterinarian may sign it.
11. Snub-nosed (brachycephalic) breeds are classified under IATA LAR Section 6.6 as requiring enhanced pre-travel health documentation. Many airlines impose additional restrictions.
12. The three-stage document sequence for the Dubai→UK route: (1) Microchip scan confirmation, (2) UAE MOCCAE health certificate, (3) UK AHC signed by OV.
13. All Emirates live cargo bookings require a signed Live Animal Checklist (IATA LAR Form) submitted at least 72 hours before departure. Late submissions are rejected.
14. Failure to present a compliant health certificate at Dubai International Airport results in refusal of boarding. There are no on-the-day exceptions.
15. New Zealand's Ministry for Primary Industries (MPI) classifies dogs from the UAE as Category 2 risk. Advance MPI approval is mandatory before the dog departs.
16. Microchip standard: ISO 11784/11785 (15-digit FDXB). Non-standard chips may not be readable at destination. Pre-flight chip verification is required.
17. The EU requires microchip implantation before — or simultaneously with — the first rabies vaccination. Post-vaccination microchipping invalidates the vaccination record.
18. Canada CBSA does not require a health certificate for dogs from the UAE, but requires proof of rabies vaccination issued within the past 3 years.

### STYLE 6: Socratic/Question-Led

**Psychological mechanism:** Information-gap theory (Loewenstein 1994, T1 [EB]). A question creates a perceived gap between what the reader knows and what they want to know — this gap is experienced as aversive, motivating the reader to read on to close it. Marvin & Shohamy 2016 operationalised this as anticipated reward (⚠️PENDING re-verify, but Loewenstein's underlying theory is well-established). Positive-valence questions outperform negative-valence ones for engagement and memory (⚠️PENDING). Best practice: ask the question your reader is already silently asking. Do not use rhetorical questions that insult the reader's intelligence.

**When to use:** Blog post openings, FAQ page headers, email subject lines, social media captions, section transitions within long-form content.

**Who it works on:** All personas in early-to-mid funnel. Highest engagement with Persona 4 (Confused Researcher) whose dominant mode is questioning.

**How to write it:** One question at a time. Lead with the word the reader is already searching (breed name, route, document type). Make the implied answer slightly surprising — creates a stronger gap.

**20 examples adapted to pet relocation:**

1. Do you know what actually gets a dog refused at Dubai airport?
2. What is the difference between a pet passport and an export health certificate — and why does it matter for your dog?
3. If you booked your flight tomorrow, would your dog be ready to fly?
4. Why do some pets get quarantined on arrival and others do not — and how do you make sure yours does not?
5. What happens if your IATA crate is 2cm too small at check-in?
6. Have you seen the full list of breeds banned from cargo? Your breed might surprise you.
7. Do you know which airlines flying Dubai to the UK accept large dogs in cargo — and which do not?
8. How long does it actually take to get a dog out of Dubai? (It is not what most websites say.)
9. What does "IATA-approved crate" actually mean — and how do you know if yours qualifies?
10. If the documentation is wrong, what actually happens to your dog at the airport?
11. Why do some pet relocation companies charge AED 5,000 and others charge AED 20,000 for the same route?
12. Why does every pet relocation website seem to say something different about the same destination country?
13. What is a titre test — and do you need one for your destination?
14. What would you do if your airline changed your flight 24 hours before departure?
15. Is it actually safe to fly a dog in cargo? Here is what the data says.
16. How do you find out if your breed is banned at your destination before it is too late?
17. What is the one document that holds up a pet export even when everything else is in order?
18. Did you know that UK quarantine for compliant pets was abolished in 2012 — and that most people still do not know this?
19. What is a "fit to fly" certificate — and which routes require one within 48 hours of departure?
20. If your dog has never flown before, what do vets actually recommend to prepare them?

### STYLE 7: Empathetic-Confessional

**Psychological mechanism:** Social proof through identification [EB: Cialdini liking principle, T1]. When the writer voices the reader's own unexpressed thought, the reader experiences cognitive recognition ("they understand me exactly") — this lowers psychological resistance and creates safety. Distinct from warm-reassuring: warm-reassuring comforts; empathetic-confessional *mirrors*. The reader feels seen before they feel helped. High-arousal negative emotions (anxiety, guilt) drive sharing [EB: Berger & Milkman 2012, T1] — this style surfaces those emotions safely.

**When to use:** Homepage hero, About Us, first-contact email sequences, WhatsApp opening messages, any page where Maya has high anxiety and low trust.

**Who it works on:** Maya (Persona 1), Persona 2 (Family), Persona 7 (Remote Relocator). Less effective with corporate buyers who prefer authority signals.

**How to write it:** Write from inside the reader's head, not from outside as an observer. "You have probably been putting this off" beats "Many clients tell us they put this off." Second person. Present tense. Acknowledge the specific emotion without amplifying the fear.

**20 examples adapted to pet relocation:**

1. You have probably been putting this off. The move is real, the flight is booked, but sorting the dog feels too complicated to start.
2. We know what the worst-case scenario looks like in your head. You have imagined it at 3am. Let us talk about it.
3. Nobody tells you how confusing this is going to be until you are already in the middle of it.
4. You are not being dramatic. Taking a dog out of Dubai really is complicated. The rules really do contradict each other online.
5. We have spoken to hundreds of people in exactly your position. The feeling is always the same: "I do not want to get this wrong."
6. Your dog is not freight to you. We understand that. That is the reason this matters so much.
7. The hardest part of the whole process for most people is not the paperwork. It is the feeling of not being in control.
8. If you have already Googled this and felt more confused afterwards, you are not alone. The information online is genuinely contradictory.
9. When you handed everything else to a removal company, it felt fine. This is different. We understand why.
10. There is no single government website that explains all of this in one place. That is why people like us exist.
11. You are worried your dog will be stressed on the flight. That is a completely normal thing to worry about. Here is what we actually know.
12. Sometimes people call us and say: "I do not even know what questions to ask." That is fine. We do.
13. If money were no issue, you would pay whatever it costs to know your dog is safe. We know that. We will not take advantage of it.
14. The guilt of leaving without a plan is worse than the cost of having one. We have heard this many times.
15. You want someone who will just tell you straight what needs to happen. So do we. That is why we built this process.
16. If you are reading this at midnight and slightly panicking — you are in the right place.
17. We have never had a client regret getting professional help with this. We have spoken to many who regretted not doing so.
18. The form that confused you for 20 minutes — we complete it 10 times a week. It takes us 5 minutes.
19. There are cheaper options. We will not pretend otherwise. The question is whether the cheaper option understood your specific route.
20. After this, you get to stop worrying about it. That is what you are actually paying for.

### STYLE 8: Urgent-Action

**Psychological mechanism:** Scarcity and loss aversion [EB: Cialdini scarcity principle; Kahneman loss framing — T1 for framing effect]. Loss-framing activates risk-avoidance behaviour more strongly than equivalent gain-framing. For this niche: urgency must be *real* — fabricated urgency in a high-trust niche destroys credibility permanently. Only deploy where the lead-time constraint genuinely exists (summer restrictions, permit timelines, titre test windows).

**When to use:** Late funnel (consideration→decision). Seasonal campaigns. Routes with genuine lead-time constraints. Abandoned inquiry follow-up. Email sequences at day 7 and day 14.

**Who it works on:** Persona 3 (Last-Minute Mover) primarily. Corporate HR relocation coordinators with fixed move dates.

**⚠️ USE SPARINGLY.** One urgency signal per page. If every page is urgent, none of them is.

## SECTION B: NEUROSCIENCE OF READING

### The retina-to-purchase pipeline

#### Stage 1: Visual input — saccades and the visual word-form area

Eyes do not scan text continuously. They jump in rapid movements (saccades, ~200ms each) with fixation pauses (~250ms average on unfamiliar words). The brain reads ahead during each fixation: typically 7–8 characters to the right of fixation are perceptually available. This has one practical implication above all others: **the left side and the first word of each line receive the most fixation time**. Front-load the key word.

The Visual Word-Form Area (VWFA, "the brain's letterbox") in the left occipital-temporal cortex handles automatic word recognition. Familiar words are processed faster — this is the neural basis for the fluency→truth pipeline confirmed below. Uncommon words, long words, and low-contrast text slow VWFA processing and increase cognitive load.

**Practical rule:** Use the most common, shortest word that carries the meaning. "Help" not "assistance." "Check" not "verify." "Dog" not "canine companion." Every uncommon word is a micro-tax on reading speed.

#### Stage 2: Processing fluency → truth → trust → willingness to buy

**CONFIRMED ✓ T1** — Processing fluency reliably shifts judgments across a broad range of social dimensions. Source: Reber, Schwarz & Winkielman 2004, PSPB.

**CONFIRMED ✓ T1** — Statements that are easier to process — via visual clarity (legible typography), conceptual priming (familiar ideas), or phonological simplicity (easy-to-say words) — are judged as MORE TRUE than harder-to-process equivalents. Source: Reber et al 2004.

**CONFIRMED ✓ T1** — Repeated exposure to a statement increases perceived truth (the "illusory truth effect"). Source: Dechêne et al 2010 meta-analysis, PSPB. Application: repeat your key safety/compliance claims across multiple pages. Consistent repetition builds credibility independent of the claim's actual novelty.

**Mechanism chain:** Easy to read → easy to process → feels true → feels trustworthy → reduces perceived risk → increases willingness to pay. Every friction point in the reading experience (confusing layout, small font, long sentences) weakens each link in this chain.

#### Stage 3: Typography — what is confirmed and what is myth

**CONFIRMED ✓ T1 (as a REFUTATION)** — Deliberately disfluent fonts (e.g., Sans Forgetica) produced NO memory advantage across four experiments and in some conditions *impaired* recall. Source: Taylor et al 2020, Memory & Cognition. The "desirable difficulty in typography" hypothesis is falsified. Do not use hard-to-read fonts to boost retention.

**What the evidence actually supports for typography:**

| Variable | Evidence level | Practical rule |
|----------|---------------|----------------|
| Font size | CONSENSUS (NN/G) | Minimum 16px body text for web; 18px preferred |
| Line length | CONSENSUS (NN/G) | 50–75 characters per line optimal |
| Line height (leading) | CONSENSUS | 1.4–1.6× font size |
| Serif vs sans-serif | ⚠️CONTESTED | No consistent advantage at screen resolutions; sans-serif slightly preferred on screen (CONSENSUS), serif slightly preferred in print |
| Contrast | T2 (WCAG + readability research) | 4.5:1 minimum contrast ratio; dark text on light background for long-form |
| Font weight | CONSENSUS | Regular for body; bold ONLY for genuinely critical information |
| ALL CAPS | CONSENSUS | Avoid for body text; 15–20% slower to read |

**The disfluency literature status (important for our evidence bank):**
- Alter & Oppenheimer 2007 originally found that disfluent fonts led to more careful System 2 thinking. This generated interest in "desirable difficulties."
- Multiple failed replications followed (2012–2020).
- Taylor et al 2020 (Sans Forgetica experiments, CONFIRMED ✓) is the clearest falsification.
- **Current status:** The disfluency hypothesis is effectively dead for typography. Fluency wins. Write and design for ease.

#### Stage 4: Dopamine, curiosity loops, and reward prediction

Curiosity operates as anticipated reward — the information gap (Loewenstein 1994) creates a state the brain wants to resolve (⚠️PENDING full verification, but T1 theoretical support). The practical application is the information-gap hook: open with a question that implies the reader does not yet have a critical piece of information they need.

**Valence finding (⚠️PENDING re-verify):** Marvin & Shohamy 2016 found positively-valenced information increased both information-seeking and memory, while negative information showed no significant advantage over neutral. **Practical implication for Maya:** Name the fear (negative valence to acknowledge), but frame the payoff positively ("your dog arrives safely" not "your dog won't be confiscated"). The fear-acknowledgement establishes relevance; the positive outcome drives engagement and memory encoding.

**Prediction error:** When content over-delivers on its promise (satisfaction > curiosity), memory encoding is stronger. Under-deliver (clickbait that resolves to thin content) and memory is weaker. Write titles that slightly undersell — then over-deliver in the body.

#### Stage 5: Oxytocin — what is now confirmed as false

**CONFIRMED ✓ T1 (as a REFUTATION)** — The claim that intranasal oxytocin administration increases human trusting behaviour has NOT replicated reliably. Source: Nave, Camerer & McCullough 2015, Perspectives on Psychological Science.

**CONFIRMED ✓ T1 (as a REFUTATION)** — The cumulative body of evidence does NOT robustly support that human trust is reliably associated with or caused by oxytocin. Same source.

**What this means for content:** Paul Zak's "trust molecule" / "story triggers oxytocin triggers trust" narrative cannot be cited. Remove any such claims from our evidence bank. Narrative does drive engagement (r = .063 small confirmed effect), but not via oxytocin. The mechanism is more likely narrative transportation (reduced counter-arguing while mentally inhabiting the story) — that mechanism is on firmer ground.

**What IS confirmed about dog-human bonding and oxytocin (different finding, different population):**
The Nagasawa et al 2015 (PNAS, source fetched) finding that mutual gaze between dogs and humans triggers an oxytocin loop in BOTH species is a separate, replicated finding about dog-human interaction — not about human-to-human trust from storytelling. This is legitimate and relevant for image science (below).

#### Stage 6: Emotional arousal → trust → purchase in high-stakes decisions

[EB: Tannenbaum 2015 meta-analysis, T1] Fear-acknowledgement + high-efficacy solution is the strongest trust mechanism (127 studies, n=27,372). This now has a mechanistic explanation from the fluency research: fear-acknowledgement reduces defensive processing (the reader stops arguing back), and the fluent delivery of the solution then runs cleanly through the fluency→trust pipeline.

The **trust meta-analysis** (50-year consumer trust review, ResearchGate source fetched) confirms trust in high-stakes services is built on three dimensions: competence, benevolence, and integrity. All three must be present — competence alone produces respect but not trust; benevolence alone produces warmth but not confidence. Our content must signal all three:
- Competence: data, regulatory precision, route knowledge (Data-Led Analytical + Authoritative-Clinical styles)
- Benevolence: we care about your dog, not just your payment (Warm-Reassuring + Empathetic-Confessional styles)
- Integrity: we tell you what can go wrong, not just what goes right (Plain-Language Instructional including limitations)

## SECTION D: IMAGE SCIENCE

### The Kindchenschema (baby schema) / cute response

Source: Nittono et al 2012 (PLoS ONE, fetched) — "The Power of Kawaii: Viewing cute images promotes careful behavior and narrows attentional focus."

**Finding:** Viewing cute images (baby animals, puppies with neotenous features — large eyes, round face, small nose) improves fine-motor performance and narrows attentional focus. The mechanism is that cute images trigger a nurturing/caregiving cognitive state that slows down and focuses attention. For a page where Maya is anxious and scanning, a high-Kindchenschema image (puppy with large eyes, soft expression) can slow her scan and shift her into a more receptive, careful-attention mode.

**Lorenz's baby schema** (Wiley ethology source, fetched): The set of features that trigger the cute response — large rounded head, large eyes relative to head size, chubby limbs, soft features. Dogs bred for companionship (Cavalier King Charles Spaniel, Golden Retriever, Labrador) score higher on these features than working breeds (German Shepherd, Doberman, Husky). **Practical: higher-Kindchenschema breeds in images → more nurturing cognitive state in reader → more receptive to trust signals.**

### Dog-human gaze and the oxytocin loop

Source: Nagasawa et al 2015 (PNAS, fetched) — "Social evolution. Oxytocin-gaze positive feedback between humans and dogs."

**Finding (replicated, T2):** Mutual gaze between dogs and humans triggers an oxytocin release in BOTH the human AND the dog. This is a genuine, replicated finding — distinct from the debunked Zak human-to-human narrative oxytocin claim. Longer mutual gaze → higher oxytocin in both. The mechanism evolved from infant-caregiver bonding and has been adapted in the dog-human relationship.

**Image implication:** A dog looking directly at the camera (toward the viewer) triggers the beginning of this gaze loop neurologically. It is not just "cute" — it activates the same social bonding pathway as looking a dog in the eyes. This is the single strongest argument for:
- Hero images where the dog faces and looks at the camera (not looking away)
- Close-up framing that shows the eyes clearly
- Soft, attentive dog expression (not fearful, not baring teeth)

### Breed perception and the snub-nosed restriction problem

**The snub-nosed issue:** Brachycephalic breeds (French Bulldog, English Bulldog, Pug, Boston Terrier, Shih Tzu, Pekingese, Cavalier King Charles Spaniel, Boxer, Brussels Griffon) are restricted or banned from cargo on most major airlines, including Emirates, Etihad, Qatar Airways, British Airways, and Lufthansa. Many are accepted only in-cabin with limits on temperature and flight duration.

**Image strategy consequence:** If our hero images feature a French Bulldog or Pug, we are showing a dog that Maya may recognise as one that cannot fly cargo on her preferred airline. This creates subconscious doubt ("but I've heard those dogs can't fly"). **Rule: default hero images should feature non-brachycephalic, medium-to-large breed dogs with high Kindchenschema scores.** Best choices: Golden Retriever, Labrador Retriever, Border Collie, Cocker Spaniel, Beagle, Cavalier King Charles Spaniel (partially brachycephalic but accepted on more routes than Bulldogs/Pugs).

**For breed-specific pages:** Show the actual breed discussed. A page about flying French Bulldogs should show a French Bulldog — the breed-specific image signals "we understand your dog specifically."

**Breed perception research (ScienceDirect consumer behaviour sources, fetched):**
Pet owners strongly identify with their breed's stereotype. A German Shepherd owner has different emotional relationship to their dog's image than a Labrador owner. Breed-matched imagery on breed-specific pages signals expertise and increases perceived relevance (Cialdini liking principle — similarity increases persuasion [EB]).

### Emotion→image mapping for fear-reduction vs trust-building

| Desired emotional state | Image characteristics | Avoid |
|------------------------|----------------------|-------|
| Fear reduction | Dog calm, relaxed in natural setting; owner relaxed alongside | Dog alone in crate; dark/enclosed spaces |
| Trust-building | Human-dog interaction showing care; process shots (vet check, handler) | Stock model interactions; glossy posed shots |
| Relief/arrival | Dog and owner reunited; outdoors; natural light; both relaxed | Airport tarmac; crates being loaded |
| Safety/competence | Handler in professional uniform showing attentiveness to animal | Empty airport scenes |
| Family belonging | Dog with child/family in natural domestic setting | Dog as cargo/logistics subject |
| Expertise/authority | Vet in professional context with calm animal | Empty clinic/lab shots |

### Image-text congruence

**Finding (source: ScienceDirect consumer behaviour and memory research, fetched):** When image and text convey the same message (congruent), recall of both is higher than when they convey different messages. This is an extension of Paivio's dual-coding theory [EB] into persuasion: congruent dual-coding amplifies both image AND text recall.

**Rule:** Never use a generic "dog being held" stock photo on a page about documentation — it is non-congruent and weakens both the image and the text. The image should answer the same question the page asks. A page about "is it safe to fly dogs in cargo?" should show a calm dog in a well-lit, clearly IATA-compliant crate setting — answering the safety question visually.

### The 50-image library recommendation

Five archetypes covering Maya's emotional journey:

1. **Relief/Reunion** (arrival gate, dog-and-owner reunited, natural light, both visibly relaxed)
2. **Process/Handling** (handler/vet showing attentive professional care, dog calm)
3. **Expertise/Credentialing** (documentation being prepared, professional context, dog present)
4. **Family/Belonging** (dog with family or child in domestic setting — not travel context)
5. **Safety/Assurance** (dog in well-lit, correctly-sized crate, calm expression, handler visible)

Breed mix target: 40% Golden Retriever/Labrador (highest Kindchenschema, safest fly status), 20% medium breeds (Spaniel, Beagle, Border Collie), 20% small breeds (non-brachycephalic: Dachshund, Jack Russell, Shih Tzu in-cabin route pages only), 10% large breeds (German Shepherd, Husky — for large-dog specific pages), 10% cats.

## SECTION E: CUSTOMER PROFILE ENRICHMENT

### Pet-as-family research

**Source: ScienceDirect pet parenting / consumer behaviour studies (2019, 2024 fetched)**

**Finding (T2):** The "pet humanisation" trend is a documented shift in consumer psychology, not a metaphor. Pet owners increasingly describe their pets using parental language, make purchase decisions based on pet welfare at parity with human family member welfare, and report higher emotional distress about pet health than about equivalent costs for non-pet family members. The key insight: **the pet is not a possession being transported — they are a family member being relocated.** This is already in Maya's ICP but now has published research support.

**Willingness-to-pay implication:** Research on high-anxiety regulated services (veterinary emergency care, and by extension pet relocation) consistently finds WTP is dominated by perceived risk reduction, not price comparison. A customer who believes a service reduces the risk of irreversible harm (pet held/confiscated) will pay a premium that exceeds rational cost-benefit calculation — because the downside is perceived as catastrophic and irreversible. **This validates our "We are not the cheapest way to move a dog" positioning.**

**Source: Animals journal (DOI 10.3390/ani15203016, 2025):**
Recent research on pet owner decision-making in regulated contexts finds that trust in the service provider's *expertise* (not just intentions) is the primary WTP driver for regulated services. Competence signals (route-specific knowledge, certification, documented outcomes) raise WTP more than warmth signals alone. This is consistent with the three-dimensional trust model (competence + benevolence + integrity) from Section B.

### Decision timeline research

**Source: PMC 12763545 (2025), ScienceDirect 1558787824000819:**

Consumer research on relocation service decisions finds two distinct decision-timeline profiles:
- **Planned relocators** (60–90 day window): spend 3–6 weeks in research mode before first contact; compare 3–5 providers; respond to authority and evidence signals; price is a decision factor but not the primary one.
- **Urgent relocators** (under 30-day window): compress research to days or hours; first provider to establish trust and demonstrate route competence earns the engagement; price sensitivity is lower under time pressure; respond to urgency + competence signals.

Maya (Persona 1, expat leaving Dubai) maps to planned relocator in most cases, but shifts to urgent-relocator behaviour when a firm departure date is set. The trigger event (job transfer confirmed, visa cancellation notice) converts a planned relocator into an urgent one overnight.

**Implication for content:** The same person may visit our site in planned-relocator mode (calm research, responds to Data-Led + Plain-Language) and return a month later in urgent-relocator mode (responds to Urgent-Action + Warm-Reassuring). Pages should serve both modes: lead with reassurance and process clarity (planned mode), and surface urgency triggers (lead time requirements, seasonal restrictions) where they are genuinely relevant.

### Expat-specific psychology

**Source: ScienceDirect 0148296319301468 (consumer trust in cross-border services):**

Expats in transition experience heightened uncertainty about regulatory systems they do not understand (a different country's rules) — this is distinct from domestic uncertainty. The combination of: unfamiliar regulatory system + irreversible consequences + physical distance from resolution options creates a trust deficit that domestic consumers do not face in the same way. This is precisely why Maya types "every website says something different" — she cannot evaluate conflicting information because she lacks the contextual knowledge to distinguish reliable from unreliable sources.

**Implication:** The trust signal that works hardest for expats is **demonstrated regulatory specificity** — not just "we know the rules" but "here is the specific UAE law, the specific UK scheme, the specific IATA section." Specificity that the reader cannot immediately Google-verify signals insider knowledge. This is the authoritative-clinical style in service of trust, not just information transfer.
