## Academic Research — Does Fear-Based Query Classification Exist?

**No.** No peer-reviewed paper proposes a fear-based search query taxonomy as of May 2026.

What does exist:
- Standard intent taxonomy (Broder 2002 — informational, navigational, transactional) still dominates
- Affective computing research classifies CONTENT and REVIEWS by emotion (GoEmotions 27-emotion taxonomy, Ekman 6 basic emotions, Plutchik's wheel) — but not search queries
- Voice-of-Customer text mining (Frey & Ananyan 2016, Taco Bell case study, 2M+ customer contacts) demonstrates emotional keyword taxonomy works at scale — but applied to customer feedback, not search queries
- A 2024 MDPI paper on fine-grained intent identification notes that existing intent schemes "lose detail" but does not propose emotion-based alternatives
- PMC 2023 study on dogs and air travel (n=635 owners) validates that fear is empirically real in this niche — finding physical and psychological harm including death — but provides no keyword data

**The gap is real.** Fear-based query classification is a category that does not exist as a published framework. You would be creating it, not copying it.

## What Standard Best Practice Looks Like in 2026

Based on Semrush, Ahrefs, Authority Hacker, Detailed.com, Search Engine Journal:

1. Start with seed topics from customer research (not keyword tools)
2. Aggregate from multiple sources: autocomplete, PAA, related searches, Search Console, competitor gap analysis, Reddit/Quora
3. Classify by standard 4-bucket intent (informational, navigational, commercial, transactional)
4. Cluster semantically using vector embeddings
5. Map one primary keyword per URL, build topic clusters
6. Iterate based on Search Console data

**Nothing in standard 2026 best practice prescribes Facebook group mining, fear-based query classification, or cross-verifying autocomplete APIs.**

## Risks and Warnings

**Facebook group mining is legally grey.** Facebook's Platform Terms restrict automated scraping. Mining private groups raises consent issues. The defensible version is manual collection in public groups where you are a member. Do not automate Facebook scraping.

**"Fear-based" framing has ethical exposure.** Engineering content to amplify fear has FTC/ASA risk in YMYL niches. Frame it as acknowledging fear and providing verified solutions — not exploiting fear. This is also E-E-A-T aligned.

**"Emotional SEO" is a noisy term.** Several low-credibility blogs write about it without methodology. Your framing should be "applied affective computing + voice-of-customer" not "emotional SEO."

**Reddit API is now paid and rate-limited since June 2023.** PRAW still works but costs and rate caps should be modeled for scale.
