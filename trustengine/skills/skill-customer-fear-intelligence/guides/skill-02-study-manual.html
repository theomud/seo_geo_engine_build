Intent Classification

Assigning the 8 intent types to every validated keyword — Column J of the spreadsheet.

Why intent matters

Every keyword in Column A represents a person at a specific moment in their journey — researching, comparing, panicking, ready to buy. Intent classification records that moment so you build the right page for the right keyword.

Get it wrong and you build a polished service page for someone who only wanted to understand the process — they bounce because they were never ready to buy. Intent fills Column J, and it must be done before fear mapping (File 03), because the fear statement depends on knowing what stage the person is in.

The 8 intent types

Not the standard four Broder buckets — these are adapted for high-stakes, regulated service markets where the usual taxonomy loses the nuance that converts.

1 · Informational

Wants to understand something. Not ready to buy — needs education.

Signals: how, what, why, guide, checklist, steps, requirements, rules
Dubai example: "how to move a dog to Dubai"
Page type: comprehensive guide — steps, timelines, document list
CTA: download checklist / subscribe — not "get a quote" yet
2 · Problem

Has a concrete obstacle to fix — something went wrong, or a specific task must be done. Not general learning (Informational), not yet an emotional worry (Fear).

Signals: failed, rejected, not accepted, "what to do if", "how to fix", next steps
Dubai example: "dog failed rabies titer test what next"
Page type: case study / problem-solution guide — document how the exact problem was solved
CTA: see how we solved this / talk to someone who has fixed this
3 · Fear

Has a specific worry that blocks progress. The highest-converting intent in regulated markets.

Signals: safe, risk, can, will, what if, denied, quarantine, confiscated
Dubai example: "is it safe to fly a dog to Dubai"
Page type: reassurance — acknowledge fear, verified answer, social proof
CTA: talk to an expert / free consultation
4 · Urgency

A real deadline approaching — days or weeks, still plannable. Time is a primary constraint, not yet a crisis.

Signals: before summer, before the embargo, this month, by [date], deadline
Dubai example: "move pet to Dubai before summer embargo"
Page type: clear action path + timeline, decisive CTA
CTA: see the timeline / book your slot now
5 · Emergency

A crisis happening now — hours, not weeks. The pet is stuck, the flight is imminent. They cannot read a long page.

Signals: stuck at airport, flight tomorrow/today, right now, stranded, asap, pet held
Dubai example: "pet stuck at Dubai airport help"
Page type: Emergency — one-line acknowledgement, immediate contact above the fold
CTA: call now / WhatsApp now — speak to someone immediately
6 · Commercial

Comparing options, moving toward a decision. Knows services exist.

Signals: best, top, compare, vs, review, cost, price, quote, company
Dubai example: "best pet relocation company Dubai"
Page type: trust-building — credentials, testimonials, pricing framework
CTA: get a quote / book a consultation
7 · Transactional

Decision made — wants to act now.

Signals: book, hire, contact, get a quote, company + location, near me
Dubai example: "pet relocation company Dubai" (no question words)
Page type: service page — clear offer, process, trust signals
CTA: get started / book now
8 · Research / Navigational

Looking for a specific brand, tool, or resource.

Signals: brand names, IPATA, specific airline, MOCCAE, a named form
Dubai example: "Blue Sky pet relocation Dubai"
Page type: comparison / alternative — be the better option, don't impersonate
CTA: why choose us instead / see how we compare

The decision process — ask in order

Run every keyword through these seven questions in sequence (most acute first). The first "yes" wins and you stop.

  1. Crisis now? (stuck at airport, flight tomorrow/today, right now, stranded) → Emergency. Stop.
  2. Deadline approaching? (before summer, before the embargo, this month, by [date]) → Urgency. Stop.
  3. Fear signals? (safe, risk, will, can, what if) → Fear. Stop.
  4. Concrete problem to fix? (failed, rejected, "what to do if", "how to fix", next steps) → Problem. Stop.
  5. Brand or named resource? (Blue Sky, Emirates, MOCCAE) → Research. Stop.
  6. Commercial signals? (best, compare, cost, price, review) → Commercial. Stop.
  7. Transactional signals? (book, hire, contact, get a quote) → Transactional. Stop.
Default → Informational. If none of Q1–Q7 fire, the person is still learning. Urgency vs Emergency: Urgency is a deadline in days/weeks (still plannable); Emergency is a crisis in progress measured in hours.

Edge cases — where human judgment is required

These are the keywords the automated classifier gets wrong. Review them manually.

Ambiguous keywordThe rule
"pet relocation Dubai cost""cost" without "quote"/"get" → Informational. With commercial context → Commercial.
"pet relocation Dubai to India" (no question word)No other signal → default Commercial. A specific route is usually past the education phase.
"safest pet relocation company Dubai"Fear + commercial → Fear wins. Address safety first, then the offer.
"Blue Sky pet relocation Dubai reviews""reviews" → Research. They're evaluating, not deciding.

Automate 80% with Claude Code

Roughly 80% of keywords classify automatically; the remaining 20% need human review. The classification prompt:

You are classifying pet relocation search keywords by intent type.
The 8 types: Informational, Problem, Fear, Urgency, Emergency, Commercial, Transactional, Research/Navigational.
For each keyword output ONLY the intent type. No explanation unless it is an edge case.

Always review by hand:

  • Every Fear classification — highest-value pages.
  • Every Emergency and Urgency classification — confirm crisis-now vs deadline-approaching; these need the fastest, most decisive pages.
  • Every Problem classification — confirm it is a concrete fix-needed keyword, not general Informational.
  • Every keyword containing a brand name.
  • Anything you personally feel uncertain about.
