Is the page built around the visitor's fear, in the 5 layers that convert?

# Measurement What to do Example (don't → do)
1 Layer 1 — fear acknowledged first Open the first 100 words on the customer's fear, in their words. DON'T: About our company. → DO: If you've been told your dog could be taken at Dubai airport — that fear is real. Here's how to prevent it.
2 Layer 2 — direct answer in the opening Give the direct answer immediately after naming the fear. DON'T: A long company intro before any answer. → DO: Yes, pets can enter Dubai year-round, but airlines suspend cargo in summer heat.
3 Layer 2 — verified fact early Add a verified, cited fact near the top. DON'T: It costs a lot. → DO: The release fee is 500 AED per dog (MOCCAE, verified 2026).
4 Layer 3 — process / steps Add the step-by-step process. DON'T: We take care of the whole process. → DO: Step 1 microchip … Step 5 fly.
5 Layer 3 — proof / evidence Add proof (official-source screenshot / real images) beside claims. DON'T: A stock image. → DO: A MOCCAE screenshot beside the rule it proves.
6 Layer 4 — related fears linked Link 2–3 related-fear pages internally. DON'T: A dead-end page with no links. → DO: Links to the 'summer embargo' and 'documentation mistakes' pages.
7 Layer 5 — help-first CTA End with a help-first CTA (checklist/guide), not 'get a quote'. DON'T: Request a consultation. → DO: Get the one-page airport checklist.
8 Layered heading structure Add H2/H3 headings to segment the page into the 5 layers. DON'T: A wall of text. → DO: H2: 'What triggers confiscation' → 'The 3 steps that prevent it'.
