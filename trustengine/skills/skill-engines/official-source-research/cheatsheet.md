# Official Source Research — Cheatsheet

*Is every claim traced to a named, dated, official source — or just asserted?*

| # | Measurement | What to do | Example (don't &rarr; do) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Official sources linked | Link the official government/authority page for each regulation/fee. | DON'T: Dubai requires documents. &rarr; DO: Dubai requires an import permit — see MOCCAE (moccae.gov.ae). |
| 2 | Authorities named | Name the actual regulator (e.g. MOCCAE, DEFRA) for each rule. | DON'T: the government says… &rarr; DO: MOCCAE (UAE) and DEFRA (UK) require… |
| 3 | Citation phrasing | Attribute claims explicitly ('according to MOCCAE…'). | DON'T: Pets need a titer test. &rarr; DO: According to MOCCAE, pets need a titer test. |
| 4 | Exact quotes | Quote the exact regulatory wording, then translate it to plain English. | DON'T: valid for about 10 days &rarr; DO: "valid for ten (10) days from the date of issue" (MOCCAE). |
| 5 | Dates / verification | Add the date each fact was verified; re-check every 90 days. | DON'T: (undated claim) &rarr; DO: Verified against MOCCAE, June 2026. |
| 6 | Figure provenance | Give every figure a source, or hedge it honestly if none is published. | DON'T: A fee figure with no source. &rarr; DO: 500 AED release fee (MOCCAE, 2026). |


**Run:** `python skill-engines/official-source-research/engine.py --url <URL>` or `--serve 8098` (web checker).
