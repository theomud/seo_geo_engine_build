## MODELS — the data structures and mental models this skill uses

- **M-21 ★ — Cialdini's 7 Principles of Persuasion.** The persuasion spine: **Reciprocity** drives the help-first CTA (give the checklist before asking for the enquiry); **Authority** and **Social Proof** are carried by the verified facts and real community quotes; **Scarcity** is used only where *real* (the summer embargo is a genuine deadline, not a manufactured one). *Source:* Cialdini, *Influence* (1984), *Pre-Suasion* (2016). *Caveat (carried from the library):* use the 6-principle spine — Unity is contested and overlaps Liking.
- **M-20 ★ — Protection Motivation Theory.** The load-bearing model. A fear appeal only produces action when severity/susceptibility are paired with **response efficacy + self-efficacy** — i.e. a credible answer the reader can act on. This is *why* Move 2 (resolve the fear in the same breath) is non-negotiable: severity alone produces avoidance. *Source:* Rogers 1975, *Journal of Psychology*; Tanner, Hunt & Eppright 1991, *Journal of Marketing* 55(3).
- **M-02 — Fear Hierarchy Model.** Ranks the named customer fears by depth so the headline and opening lead with the *deepest* one for the page (confiscation > paperwork > cost). Move 1 selects from this hierarchy in the customer's own words. *Source:* maps to the PMT severity hierarchy (Rogers 1975).

*Also used:* **M-01** Verified Fact (every claim sourced or hedged) · **M-07** Five-Layer Page Model (the opening *is* layers 1–2: fear → verified answer) · **M-10** Ten-Criteria Quality Model (the /50 editorial gate the copy must clear).

## FRAMEWORKS — the decision systems and processes

- **F-19 — PAS (Problem · Agitate · Solution).** The structural spine of the fear-acknowledging opening — but with a hard rule layered on by P-04: the **Solution must arrive in the same passage as the Agitation**. We use PAS to *resolve*, never to leave the reader agitated. *Source:* direct-response canon (CrazyEgg / SaaSFunnelLab).
- **F-18 — AIDA (Attention · Interest · Desire · Action).** Attention is won by naming the real fear in the headline; Action is the help-first CTA. *Source:* E. St. Elmo Lewis, 1898. *Caveat (carried from the library):* AIDA is a writing scaffold, not a validated buyer-behaviour model (Vakratsas & Ambler 1999) — used for structure, not as proof of how buyers think.
- **F-20 — Ogilvy's AICPBSAWN.** Ogilvy's long-form persuasion sequence (Attention, Interest, Credibility, Proof, Benefits…) — the source of this skill's insistence on **Credibility and Proof early**, which in a regulated market means a C-ID beside the claim, not adjectives. *Source:* David Ogilvy, *Ogilvy on Advertising*.

*Also used:* **F-32** Funnel-Stage Copy Matching (AIDA at awareness, PAS at consideration) · **F-08** Proof Interstitial (the verified fact sits *beside* the fear it resolves).

## PRINCIPLES — the non-negotiable rules

- **P-04 — Fear-Acknowledging Not Fear-Exploiting.** *The* governing principle of this skill. Every fear raised is resolved in the same breath; a page that agitates without answering is a failure regardless of its other scores. *Source:* PMT — fear without efficacy produces denial/avoidance, not action (Rogers 1975).
- **P-11 — Real Examples Mandatory.** The fear-acknowledging opening is built from a **named real quote or a Column K fear**, never invented emotion; every benefit is a real number/name. "Being quoted endless amount" (7Ssisi) beats any copywriter's paraphrase. *Source:* Ogilvy — "no substitute for homework."
- **P-13 ⟳ — Hormozi Test Before Publishing.** The help-first CTA must pass *"would you rather receive this or get a quote?"* — and the whole page must be one you'd put your name on. *Update:* paired with Ogilvy's "homework" so it isn't single-author lineage. *Source:* Hormozi; Ogilvy.

*Also used:* **P-03** Proof Over Promise (show, don't tell — every claim carries proof or is cut) · **P-16** Ban Generic Language (the brochure phrases — "navigating the complexities", "your trusted partner" — are prohibited, the failure mode the voice score catches).
