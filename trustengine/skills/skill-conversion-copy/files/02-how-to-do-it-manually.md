# Skill · File 02 — How To Do It Manually
## Writing the headline, the fear-acknowledging opening, and the help-first CTA

---

## Why manual first

Conversion copy is a judgement of *voice*. Automation can draft ten openings and
pre-score them, but "does this sound like a real person who has done this, or like
a brochure?" stays human until your ear is trained. Write the three moves by hand
for several pages until the pattern is automatic — then the engine (File 04) drafts
fast and you keep the judgement.

You need two inputs on the desk before you write a single word:
1. **The named fear** for this page — in the customer's own language (Column K / a
   real quote). If you can't name the fear in their words, stop and go get it.
2. **The verified facts** that resolve it — by C-ID from the Source Bank, with the
   honest hedge noted where no official figure exists.

Write nothing you cannot trace to one of those two.

---

## Step 1 — Write the fear-acknowledging opening (the hardest, do it first)

The opening is 1–3 sentences. Move 1: say the fear out loud, in their words. Move 2:
resolve it immediately with a verified answer or an honest hedge. *(Library: P-04
Fear-Acknowledging Not Fear-Exploiting; M-20 Protection Motivation Theory — a fear
stated without a credible resolution produces avoidance, not action.)*

The opening is **not done** until it scores **4+/5** on the in-the-trenches voice
criterion:

| Voice score | What it means |
|-------------|---------------|
| 5 | Uses real customer language traceable to a named quote/Column K fear; specific; a person who has lived this wrote it |
| 4 | Clearly customer-voiced and specific; minor polish would lift it |
| 3 | Generic-but-acceptable; could be sharper; no real language yet |
| 2 | Brochure voice; abstract; "navigating the complexities of…" |
| 1 | Pure AI filler; could front any company's page |

**Rule:** if the opening agitates the fear but does **not** resolve it in the same
breath, it fails P-04 — it is fear-*exploiting*. Cut it.

---

## Step 2 — Write the headline (from the resolved fear)

The H1 names the fear and promises the verified answer — a question the reader is
already asking, or the answer they are desperate for. *(Library: F-19 PAS; F-18
AIDA — Attention via the named fear.)*

| Weak (cut) | Strong (keep) |
|------------|---------------|
| "Pet Relocation Services in Dubai" | "What happens if your dog is taken at Dubai airport — and how to make sure it never does" |
| "Affordable Titer Testing" | "How much the rabies titer test really costs in Dubai (the 700–1,300 AED range, sourced)" |

A headline that could sit on a competitor's brochure has failed. It must be
answerable only because *this* page did the homework.

---

## Step 3 — Write the help-first CTA (offer before ask)

Move 3. The CTA offers the thing the scared owner actually wants, *before* asking
for the enquiry. *(Library: P-13 Hormozi Test; M-21 Cialdini — Reciprocity.)*

It is **not done** until it passes the test: **"would you rather receive this, or
get a quote?"** — a frightened owner must genuinely prefer the offered thing.

| Fails the test (Dead End) | Passes the test (help-first) |
|---------------------------|------------------------------|
| "Contact us for a quote" | "Get the one-page checklist that stops your dog being turned away at the counter" |
| "Request a callback" | "Download the summer-embargo calendar — the dates no airline puts on its homepage" |
| "Get started today" | "See the real Sharjah vs Dubai vs Abu Dhabi routing table before you book a flight" |

One primary CTA per page. The enquiry comes *after* the help is delivered.

---

## Step 4 — Map every claim to a source

Beside each factual statement in the copy, write the C-ID (e.g. C-019) or mark it
`HEDGE` with the honest wording. *(Library: M-01 Verified Fact; P-03 Proof Over
Promise.)* No bare scare, no unsourced number. Where the Source Bank has no official
figure — titer cost has none — the copy says so: *"vets and agents quote 700–1,300
AED; there is no official published price, so get it in writing before you pay."*
That honesty converts this buyer better than a confident fake number.

---

## Step 5 — Score against the threshold and decide

Run the page's copy through the Editorial 10-criteria rubric (File 02 of Editorial
Judgment). The copy is done when **all three** hold:

1. **40+/50** on the editorial rubric, nothing below 3.
2. Opening scores **4+/5** on voice.
3. CTA passes the help-first test.

Miss any one → rewrite. Record the before/after, the scores, and the C-ID map in
`data/conversion-copy-output.md`.

---

## Worked example — the airport-confiscation page

**Named fear (Column K, "Rejection/denial"; real quote):** Muze Gu, Facebook "Dog
Lovers In UAE" — *"without it your dog will be taken away in airport and never give
back from you… Still when I remember I crying."*

**Opening (Move 1 + 2):**
> *"The fear that keeps you up isn't the paperwork — it's the thought of your dog
> being taken at the airport and not given back. It happens, and it happens for one
> reason: a missing or invalid rabies titer test. Here is exactly what the document
> must say, and what to do if you're ever stopped at the counter."*

Voice score: **5/5** — built from Muze Gu's own words; specific cause named; resolves
in the same breath (P-04 satisfied). Claims map to the titer requirement (C-ID from
Source Bank) with the rejection/release process hedged where MOCCAE is silent.

**Headline:**
> *"What happens if your dog is taken at Dubai airport — and the one document that
> stops it"*

**Help-first CTA (Move 3):**
> *"Get the one-page airport checklist — the exact titer wording and the three things
> to carry to the counter — before your flight is booked."*

CTA test: **passes** — a scared owner wants that checklist more than a quote.

Editorial score (from the proven Editorial run): **47/50 → publish.** This is the
page 9/9 competitors never write, because they avoid the fear instead of resolving it.

---

## What you must not do

- **Do not invent the fear.** If it isn't in a Column K fear or a real quote, you
  don't have a fear yet — you have a guess.
- **Do not agitate without resolving.** Fear + no answer = avoidance (P-04). It is
  the one unforgivable error in this skill.
- **Do not write a number you can't source.** Cite a C-ID or hedge honestly.
- **Do not close with "Contact us for a quote."** If the CTA fails the help-first
  test, it is not finished.
- **Do not let voice drop below 4/5** to sound "professional." Brochure voice is the
  failure mode, not the safe choice.

## Output of this manual phase

Each gap page has: a rewritten headline, a fear-acknowledging opening, and a
help-first CTA; a /50 editorial score, a voice score, a CTA-test result; and a C-ID
source map. The four universal-gap pages done end-to-end are the real output
(`data/conversion-copy-output.md`) and the input to File 04 — the engine drafts and
pre-scores; the human still owns the voice call.
