Conversion Copy

The actual words on the page — headline, opening, CTA — that turn a scared reader into an enquiry. Without exploiting the fear, because this buyer distrusts hype on sight.

Why this skill

A page can be true, well-structured, and still convert nobody. The reader arrives afraid — they are about to move a living animal across a border where one paperwork error means refusal at the counter — and the page talks to them in the flat voice of a brochure. They leave and ask a Facebook group instead.

You can hear the fear in their own words from the Dubai research:

"without it your dog will be taken away in airport and never give back… Still when I remember I crying" — Muze Gu (Facebook, deepest fear in the dataset)  ·  "being quoted endless amount" — 7Ssisi  ·  "I felt like they took advantage of the desperate circumstances" — unnnabear (Reddit, 7↑)

Conversion Copy is the skill that writes the words that meet that fear and resolve it. It takes two inputs and nothing else: the named fear in the customer's words, and the verified fact that answers it.

It is not "salesy" copywriting. In a high-fear, regulated market, hype repels. The writing that converts is the writing that acknowledges the fear and resolves it with a verified answer, then offers help.

The three moves

Every page is built from the same three moves, in this order:

2 · Resolve itanswer it immediately with a verified fact or honest hedgeM-20 PMT, M-01 Verified Fact

The whole skill is scorable: every opening must hit 4+/5 on voice, every page must clear 40+/50 on the editorial rubric, and every CTA must pass the help-first test.

Move 1 · name the fear

Pull the exact phrasing from real community language — never invent the emotion. The deepest fear in the proof niche isn't "import compliance"; it's Muze Gu's "taken away… never give back." Score the opening on the in-the-trenches voice rubric:

VoiceMeans
5real customer language traceable to a named quote; specific; a person who lived it wrote it
4clearly customer-voiced and specific; minor polish would lift it
3generic-but-acceptable; no real language yet
2brochure voice — "navigating the complexities of…"
1pure AI filler; could front any company's page
Voice 1 — brochure
"Navigating the complexities of international pet travel can be challenging."
Voice 5 — real
"The fear that keeps you up isn't the paperwork — it's the thought of your dog being taken at the airport and not given back."

Move 2 · resolve it

This is the load-bearing move. Protection Motivation Theory (M-20): a fear raised without a credible, actionable answer produces denial and avoidance — not action. So every fear is resolved immediately, with a fact cited by C-ID or an honest hedge.

The one unforgivable error: agitating the fear without resolving it. "Your dog could be seized!" with no answer is fear-exploiting — it breaches P-04 and, in this market, does real harm. Cut it every time.

Where the Source Bank has no official figure, the honesty is the conversion move:

"There is no official published price for the rabies titer test. What owners actually pay runs roughly 700–1,300 AED, and the difference is mostly lab turnaround." C-001 · hedge

Move 3 · help-first CTA

Offer the thing the scared owner actually wants before asking for the enquiry (Cialdini reciprocity, M-21). The test: "would you rather receive this, or get a quote?" — they must genuinely prefer the offer.

Fails — Dead End
"Contact us for a quote"
"Request a callback"
"Get started today"
Passes — help-first
"Get the one-page checklist that stops your dog being turned away"
"Download the summer-embargo calendar"
"See the real airport routing table before you book"
For this buyer, "get a quote" actively raises the fear it should lower — it reminds them of being "quoted endless amount." One primary CTA per page; the enquiry follows the help.

The catch that proves it

The clearest proof is the titer-cost page. The market's default version — and what an unguarded AI writes — looks helpful but contains the dangerous error:

BEFORE — fabricated fact
"We provide competitive pricing… The titer test costs 700 AED. Contact us for a quote."
A single price asserted as fact with no source. In a market where wrong info gets a pet refused, this is harm, not a typo.
AFTER — honest, converts harder
"There is no official published price… owners pay roughly 700–1,300 AED… here's how to get it in writing." C-001 hedge
Zero invented numbers. The refusal to fake a figure is the trust move.
The surprise from the build: we expected the honest version (no firm number) to convert worse. It scores higher — 48/50 — because for a reader who's been "quoted endless amount," honesty about the uncertainty is exactly what earns the click. That is what conversion copy is for.

Acknowledging, not exploiting

The failure mode is copy that frightens without answering, or invents a number to force urgency. Three defences:

  • Every fear traces to a named quote or a Column K fear — no invented emotion.
  • Every number is cited (C-ID) or honestly hedged — no fabricated figures.
  • An independent blind re-score must agree the copy resolves the fear (acknowledging), not merely raises it (exploiting); voice within ±1; CTA pass/fail must match.

The batch audit re-scores 20% blind; ≥90% must agree, with zero invented numbers and zero agitate-without-resolve openings slipping through.
