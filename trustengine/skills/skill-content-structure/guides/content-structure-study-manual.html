Content Structure for Trust

Page architecture that converts fear into action. Most frameworks organise around the topic. This one organises around the fear behind the topic.

Why this skill exists

In regulated, high-stakes markets — pet relocation, immigration, medical, legal — customers do not arrive curious. They arrive afraid. A page that opens with "Welcome to our services" speaks past that fear and loses them in the first sentence. Content Structure is not a writing style; it is a structural system that decides what appears in the first 100 words, where the proof sits, and what the CTA asks for. The structure is fixed; the content inside it changes per niche.

The test of a good page: the first sentence names the customer's real fear in their own words — and every claim after it is backed by a verified source.

The 5-layer structure

Every page is built in five layers, each earning a different kind of trust. Real examples below are from the four pages built in the Dubai proof run.

Layer 1 · Fear acknowledgement (first 100 words)

Name the customer's exact fear in their language. Builds emotional trust.

"without it your dog will be taken away in airport and never give back… Still when I remember I crying" — Muze Gu (used to open the airport-confiscation page)
Layer 2 · Verified answer

Answer the fear; every claim cites a Verified Source Bank row. Builds informational trust.

"You apply for the import permit online, before travel." Verified C-019 · MOCCAE · "release fee 500 AED per dog." Verified C-003
Layer 3 · Process / evidence

Show the proof — an official-source screenshot beside the claim. Builds credibility trust.

The cost-transparency page embeds the MOCCAE page itself as proof it is silent on the titer cost — the absence is the evidence.
Layer 4 · Related fears

Link 2–3 neighbouring fears. Builds completeness trust.

The confiscation page links → document checklist, titer-cost page, summer-embargo page.
Layer 5 · Help-first CTA

Offer something useful, not a sales ask. Builds relational trust.

Good: "Download the Dubai pet-import checklist — verified against MOCCAE."   Bad: "Get a Quote."
Order is load-bearing: you cannot earn informational trust (L2) from a reader whose fear you never acknowledged (L1).

The Intent × Fear matrix

Intent (Column J, 8 types) + fear (Column K) = page type, in four seconds. No deliberation — the matrix decides.

Fear + Confiscation → Fear Resolution · Informational + Overwhelm → Process Guide · Commercial + Price gouging → Cost Transparency · Research + Wrong provider → Comparison

The 8 intent types: Informational · Problem · Fear · Urgency · Emergency · Commercial · Transactional · Research.

The Proof Interstitial framework

Proof must sit beside each claim, woven through the page — not collected into one testimonials block at the bottom. This is the criterion Hormozi's analysis sharpened.

Passes: the MOCCAE official-source screenshot embedded beside the 500 AED release-fee claim on the confiscation page (C-003).
Fails: a single "what our clients say" block and stock photos. Proof in one section ≠ proof interstitial.

Silence is also proof: the cost-transparency page embeds the full MOCCAE page to show it says nothing about the titer cost — proving any "official price" quote is not official (C-001).

Source Bank integration — by C-ID

Every factual claim traces to a row in the verified Source Bank, cited by its claim ID. Three outcomes the writer must handle differently:

Verified → assert it (with the citation)

"The import permit is valid for 90 days." Verified C-010 — state it plainly and link the source.

Unverifiable → hedge it, never assert

Titer-test cost: MOCCAE publishes no figure. Write: "Community reports 700–1,300 AED; no official figure exists." C-001

Conflicting → lead with the official value, name the discrepancy

Community said the Etihad cabin fee was USD 1,500; the official page shows USD 399. Conflict C-015 — a customer-protecting finding.
Verified before published: no factual claim ships without a Source Bank row whose status is Verified — not Unverifiable, not Pending.

The Hormozi test

Before a page ships, three questions decide it:

  1. Would you put your name on it? If not — what specifically must change?
  2. Is the proof undeniable and real-world? A dated official-source screenshot beats any adjective.
  3. Could AI alone have written it? If yes, it lacks the in-the-trenches specifics (real C-IDs, real quotes, real conflicts) that build trust.

Proof density rule: higher risk demands more proof. Dubai pet relocation is maximum risk — every claim traces to an official source or is openly hedged.
