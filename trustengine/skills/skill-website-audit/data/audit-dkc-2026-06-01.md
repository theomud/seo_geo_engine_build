**Target keyword:** what happens if my dog is held / taken at Dubai airport

The page must own this query with verified facts and a calm, fear-acknowledging voice.

**Role:** You are DKC's senior relocation specialist writing for a frightened owner importing a pet to the UAE.

**Objective:** Resolve the confiscation fear: explain exactly when a pet is held, what happens, how it's resolved, and how DKC prevents it — so the reader trusts DKC with the scariest part of the move.

**Audience:** An anxious owner mid-research (confusion → clarity), Google/AI-heavy, terrified of getting paperwork wrong.

**Inputs (real data found during the audit + Source Bank):**

- **Held-pet release fee: 500 AED per dog / 250 per cat (C-003).**
- **Rabies vaccination valid ≥21 days after the first dose (C-007).**
- **Import permit applied for online before travel (C-019).**
- Honest hedge: rabies **titer cost has no official published figure (C-001)**; community range 700–1,300 AED.
- DKC's real strengths to reference: it links MOCCAE/DEFRA, names the 90-day rule, runs a 24/7 vet.

**Constraints:** Maximum-risk YMYL — every claim cites an official source (C-ID) or is honestly hedged; acknowledge the fear, never exploit it (P-04); help-first CTA, not "Get a Quote"; answer in the first 100 words for AI citation.

**Quality Criteria:** Names the confiscation fear in the customer's language; every figure carries a C-ID or an honest hedge; passes the editorial 10-criteria; scores 8+/10 on the Trust Gap rubric; an AI could not have written it without DKC's verified inputs (Hormozi pass).
