## Only the excerpts an auditor needs to score a site's 13 dimensions

## Why this matters for an audit

A website audit is only as honest as the standard you hold the site to — and that standard comes from
the *customer*, not from web-design taste. You are not scoring whether a page looks nice; you are
scoring whether it earns the trust of a frightened person making a high-stakes, time-boxed decision.
So before scoring any dimension you need three things from the profile: **the risk level** (it sets the
proof bar every claim must clear — M-04), **the fears** (D1 Fear Intelligence and D6 Conversion Copy
score whether the site names them or ignores them), and **the verified facts** (D3 Source Verification
scores whether the site's claims are cited to official sources or asserted). Everything else in the
master profile is unnecessary for an audit.

---

## The risk level → the proof bar (calibrate before scoring)

This customer is moving a pet internationally: a **maximum-risk (YMYL)** decision where wrong
information means a pet denied entry or held at the border. *(Library: M-04 Risk Continuum.)* That
sets the bar for the whole audit: on a maximum-risk site, **every factual claim must be backed by an
official source (P-02, P-07)** — an unsupported number is not a minor flaw, it is a scoring failure.
Calibrate M-04 first; a site that would pass at low risk fails here.

| Risk level | Proof bar the audit applies |
|------------|-----------------------------|
| Maximum (this niche) | Official source required for **every** claim; wrong info = real harm |
| Med-High | Source required for safety/money claims |
| Med-Low / Low | Reasonable sourcing; design-trust signals carry more weight |

## The customer state → what "good" looks like on the page

The buyer is **Google-heavy, fear-driven, in a "confusion → clarity" state** — increasingly arriving via
an AI answer (ChatGPT/Perplexity/AI Overview) rather than ten blue links. They trust the source that is
most specific, most current, and most honest about what is and isn't official. This is the lens for
D1 (does the homepage/service page open by naming the fear?), D6 (does the copy acknowledge the fear
without exploiting it — P-04?), and D11 (is the page the AI would cite?).

## The fears (Column K) → the test for Dimensions 1 and 6

A site scores well on Fear Intelligence and Conversion Copy only if it **names these fears in the
customer's own language and answers them** — not if it buries them under "your pet is family"
marketing. The recurring high-stakes fears to check the site against:

| Fear | Verbatim signal | Audited in |
|------|-----------------|------------|
| Confiscation at the airport | *"without it your dog will be taken away… Still when I remember I crying"* (Muze Gu) — the deepest fear in the market | D1, D6, D9 |
| Price gouging | *"shamelessly charging an insane amount"* (IrbisKat); *"being quoted endless amounts"* (7Ssisi) | D1, D6 |
| Exploited in a crisis | *"took advantage of the desperate circumstances"* (unnnabear) | D1, D6 |
| Wrong airport / document mistake | which of Dubai / Sharjah / Abu Dhabi, and the paperwork | D1, D4 |
| Heat embargo / timeline failure | summer cargo ban, missed window | D1, D4 |
| Breed rejection | BSH rejected by Emirates (a real case) | D1 |

**The benchmark finding:** the gap matrix shows the airport-confiscation fear is missing on **9/9**
scored competitors — *including the market leader DKC (8.0/10)*. An audit that doesn't catch a site
ignoring its market's deepest fear isn't auditing trust.

## The verified facts (C-IDs) → the test for Dimension 3

Source Verification scores whether the site's claims are cited to an official source or merely
asserted. Hold the site's numbers against the verified Source Bank rows; an uncited or wrong figure on
a maximum-risk site is a D3 failure (P-02). The anchor facts:

| Fact | Status | C-ID |
|------|--------|------|
| Import permit applied for online before travel | Verified | C-019 |
| Import permit valid **90 days** | Verified | C-010 |
| Rabies vaccination valid ≥**21 days** after first dose | Verified | C-007 |
| Held-pet release fee **500 AED/dog** (250/cat) | Verified | C-003 |
| flydubai **cargo-only** (no cabin) | Verified | C-022 |
| Etihad fee **USD 399** official vs **~USD 1,500** community | Verified conflict | C-015 |
| Rabies titer price | **No official figure** (community 700–1,300 AED) | C-001 |

## How to use this snapshot

Calibrate the risk level (M-04) → score D1/D6 by checking whether the site names the fears above in the
customer's language → score D3 by re-tracing the site's claims to a verified C-ID (or flagging the
uncited/asserted ones) → benchmark D2/D13 against DKC's 8.0/10 and the 9/9 gap. For a new niche, swap
this table for that market's fears and verified facts; the scoring method is identical.
