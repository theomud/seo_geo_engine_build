# Skill · File 01 — What This Skill Is
## Niche-agnostic definition of the Website Audit

---

## The problem this skill solves

A business owner asks "is my website any good?" and the tools answer the wrong question. Lighthouse
grades page speed. An SEO crawler counts meta tags and broken links. A heatmap shows where people
click. None of them answer the question that actually decides whether the site makes money: **does a
frightened, high-stakes visitor trust this enough to act — and if not, what is the single
highest-leverage thing to fix first?** Surface audits are cheap and everywhere; a *trust* audit
graded against a complete, evidence-backed standard barely exists.

The Website Audit solves it by turning the **entire 13-skill Trust Engine into one scoring
instrument**. Every skill in the system — fear intelligence, source verification, editorial quality,
conversion copy, visual evidence, authority, AI citation, and the rest — becomes one **dimension
scored out of 10**, backed by **evidence quoted from the live site**, totalling a **130-point**
score. The gaps are then ranked by **RICE** so the owner knows exactly what to do first, and the
single most urgent missing page is handed over as a **ready-to-execute content brief**. It is the
difference between "your site scores 71/100 on performance" and "your site never names the deepest
fear your customers have, here is the page to build, and here is the prompt that writes it."

---

## What this skill produces

| Output | What it is |
|--------|-----------|
| Executive summary | Overall **/130** + market position, top-3 strengths, top-3 gaps, single most important action |
| 13 dimension scores | Each **/10**, with status and detailed findings backed by site-quoted evidence |
| RICE-ranked action plan | Every gap scored `(Reach × Impact × Confidence) ÷ Effort`, in priority order |
| Content brief | A complete 9-element prompt for the highest-priority missing page |
| Competitor comparison | The same 13 dimensions side-by-side against named competitors (optional) |

The rule that makes it scorable: **all 13 dimensions carry a /10 score with evidence quoted from the
live site, every gap is RICE-scored, at least one ready-to-execute content brief is produced, and the
executive summary passes the client-ready test** (a business owner could act on it unaided).

---

## The core idea — score trust, with evidence, then prioritise by consequence

A trust audit is not a checklist a script can tick; it is an act of **evidenced judgement**. Three
principles make it rigorous rather than opinion:

1. **Calibrate the proof bar first (M-04 Risk Continuum).** A maximum-risk site (health, money, a
   pet's life) must cite an official source for *every* claim; a low-risk site need not. Score against
   the right bar, or the whole audit is miscalibrated.
2. **Every score carries evidence quoted from the live site (P-03 Proof Over Promise; P-07
   Independent Verification).** "Dimension 1: 3/10" means nothing; "Dimension 1: 3/10 — the homepage
   opens *'Where family flies together'* and never names the confiscation fear" is a finding the owner
   can act on. No quoted evidence, no score.
3. **Rank the fixes by consequence, not by ease (M-27 RICE; F-05 Dashboard-to-Action).** Thirteen
   dimensions surface more gaps than anyone can fix at once; RICE turns them into one ordered queue so
   the highest-leverage fix is unambiguous.

The parent model is **E-E-A-T with Trust at the centre (M-24, P-22)** — the other three pillars
collapse without it — and the whole instrument is the **45/47-check audit discipline (F-11)** pointed
outward at a live site.

---

## The 13 dimensions (one per Trust Engine skill)

| # | Dimension | Scores whether the site… |
|---|-----------|--------------------------|
| 1 | Fear Intelligence | names its market's real fears in the customer's language |
| 2 | Trust Gap Score | clears the 10-point page trust score (fear-first, sourced, route, steps, timeline, cost, mistakes, visuals, help-CTA, proof) |
| 3 | Source Verification | cites every factual claim to an official source (or hedges honestly) |
| 4 | Content Structure | structures pages fear → answer → proof → related → help-CTA |
| 5 | Editorial Quality | clears the 10-criteria editorial rubric (no weak AI patterns) |
| 6 | Conversion Copy | acknowledges the fear without exploiting it; help-first CTAs |
| 7 | Visual Evidence | shows real screenshots / photography / data, not stock |
| 8 | Site Architecture | is navigable, ≤3 clicks, clean URLs, no orphans |
| 9 | Authority Assets | carries case studies / guides that pass the Hormozi test |
| 10 | Email Nurture | captures email and nurtures with fear-named, help-first sequences |
| 11 | AI Citation | is answer-first with schema + entity — the page an AI would cite |
| 12 | Monitoring Evidence | shows freshness / accuracy / responsiveness signals |
| 13 | Market Position | is differentiated, scores well vs market, and is AI-visible |

## A worked example (the proof niche)

The proof run audits **DKC (Dubai Kennels & Cattery — dkc.ae)**, the Dubai pet-relocation benchmark
that scored 8.0/10 on the prior 10-point Trust Gap Analysis. The audit visits the live site and scores
all 13 dimensions with quoted evidence — and the headline finding is the one the framework is built to
catch: even the **market leader never names the airport-confiscation fear** (the Muze Gu scenario), the
deepest fear in the market and the gap missing on 9/9 competitors. That becomes the
highest-RICE action and the content brief the audit hands back. A surface audit would have called DKC's
site "clean and professional"; the trust audit finds the single page that would move the market.

## How it differs from neighbouring skills

| Skill | Owns |
|-------|------|
| Trust Gap Analysis | scores **competitors** on the **10-point** trust score to find market gaps |
| Editorial Judgment | scores **one page's** copy on the 10-criteria quality rubric |
| Customer Fear Intelligence | *produces* the fears that this audit checks a site against |
| **Website Audit** | scores **one whole site** on **all 13 skills (130 pts)** with evidence + a prioritised plan |

Trust Gap Analysis looks *outward* at the field on one dimension; Website Audit looks at *one site* on
*every* dimension and ends in an action plan and a content brief. The other skills each own one lens or
produce one input; this skill is the **instrument that runs all of them at once** and turns the result
into something a client can act on.

---

## Why this is a standalone skill

1. **It's a sellable deliverable, not a metric.** *(Commercial 5/5.)* A 130-point, evidence-backed,
   client-ready audit with a prioritised plan is a product a business will pay for; a Lighthouse score
   is not.
2. **Nothing on the market combines all 13 lenses.** *(Uniqueness 5/5.)* Surface audits grade
   mechanics; this grades *trust*, against a complete evidence-backed framework, in a YMYL market where
   trust is the whole game.
3. **It's portable and teachable.** *(Teachability 5/5.)* Every market has a website, a trust bar, and
   competitors; the 13 dimensions and the score-with-evidence-then-RICE method transfer cleanly — only
   the fears and verified facts change.

---

## In scope / out of scope

**In scope.** Scoring a site's 13 trust dimensions /10 with quoted evidence; calibrating the proof bar
(M-04); RICE-ranking the gaps; writing the executive summary and the highest-priority content brief;
the optional competitor comparison on the same 13 dimensions.

**Out of scope.** *Originating* the verified facts (Official Source Research owns the C-IDs) or the
fears (Customer Fear Intelligence owns Column K); *building* the pages the action plan recommends (the
content/copy skills); technical performance metrics (page speed, Core Web Vitals) beyond how they
affect the trust dimensions.

---

## What "good" looks like

- **All 13 dimensions scored /10 with evidence quoted from the live site** — no unscored dimension, no
  score without a quote.
- **The proof bar calibrated to the site's risk level** before scoring (M-04).
- **Every gap RICE-scored** and ordered; the single most important action named with a specific page.
- **At least one ready-to-execute content brief** for the top gap, filled with real data from the audit.
- **A client-ready executive summary** — a business owner could read it and act without the framework.

This skill is complete when an audit of a real site meets all four threshold gates and the audit
passes. The proof run is the live DKC audit in `data/audit-dkc-2026-06-01.md`.
