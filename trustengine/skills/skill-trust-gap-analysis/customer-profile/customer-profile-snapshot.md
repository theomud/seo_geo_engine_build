## Why this matters for Trust Gap Analysis

The customer is **not buying transport, permits, or logistics — they are buying certainty, compliance, safety, and emotional relief.** The dog is family. The winner in this market is *not* the company with the most keywords; it is the one that makes the owner feel **"They understand my situation, and they know exactly what needs to happen."**

Decision style: **cautious, emotional, comparison-driven — but acts quickly once trust is established.** That single sentence is why this skill exists: the customer compares providers and commits to the most-trusted one. Every dimension a competitor fails on the 10-Point Trust Score is a reason this customer keeps comparing instead of committing. **A trust gap is a lost sale.**

## The fears competitors fail to address → the universal gaps

The four universal gaps (missed by 9/9 scored competitors) map directly to the deepest documented customer fears:

1. **Airport confiscation** — *the deepest fear in the dataset:*
   > "Please do all the paperwork especially important rabies titers without it your dog will be taken away in airport and never give back from you my friends had the worst experience. Still when I remember I crying" — Muze Gu, Facebook "Dog Lovers In UAE"
   No competitor addresses it directly → the highest-priority gap.
2. **Price gouging** — > "Been quoted some ridiculous prices from companies and vets" / "relocation companies are shamelessly charging an insane amount of money" (IrbisKat, 24 upvotes) → the cost-transparency gap (dimension 6).
3. **Who to trust** — > "Every website says something different — I don't know who to trust" → the official-source-citation gap (dimension 2) and proof-interstitial gap (dimension 10).
4. **Timeline / embargo** — > "We are moving soon. Is there still time?" → the titer-cost-and-timeline and summer-embargo gaps (dimension 5).

## How to use this when scoring

For each competitor page, score the 10 dimensions **as this customer would experience them**: does the opening name the fear they actually arrived with? Does the page prove its claims, or ask them to trust on faith? In a maximum-risk niche, a polished page that resolves none of these fears is a 3–5/10 — which is why the Dubai market average is 3.9/10 and the confiscation fear (the deepest of all) is unaddressed by every competitor scored.
