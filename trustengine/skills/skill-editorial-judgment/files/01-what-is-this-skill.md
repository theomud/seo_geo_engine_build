# Skill · File 01 — What This Skill Is
## Niche-agnostic definition of Editorial Judgment & Quality Control

---

## The problem this skill solves

AI produces text fast. But most AI content **fails**: it is generic, it sounds fake, and it does not convert. Publish it and you have spent budget building pages that earn no trust and no ranking — in a regulated market, that is worse than nothing.

Editorial Judgment is the quality-control gate that transforms AI output from ~60% quality to **90%+ before anything is published**. It is **not proofreading.** Proofreading fixes grammar; this decides whether content will *rank, convert, and build trust* — and exactly what to remove, what to add, and whether the result is strong enough to ship. It is the discipline that stands between a fast first draft and a page you would put your name on.

---

## What this skill produces

| Output | What it is |
|--------|-----------|
| Edited, publish-ready content | The draft, fixed against the 10 criteria |
| A quality score out of 50 | Each of the 10 criteria scored /5 — the publish gate |
| Edit notes | What was changed and why — the record that makes the judgement auditable |

A page does not ship until it scores **40+/50** and clears the publishing checklist.

---

## The 10-criteria quality framework

Score every page on all ten (the order is the rubric): **True · Specific · Useful · Clear · Commercial · Trustworthy · Better-than-competitors · Brand-aligned · Written-for-the-right-buyer · Strong-enough-to-publish.** *(Library: M-10 Ten-Criteria Quality Model; depth judged via M-11; "True" enforced by M-01 Verified Fact.)*

The two that catch the most AI failures: **Specific** (could this sentence appear on any business's site? then it fails — "many happy customers" fails; "4.8 stars from 283 Google reviews" passes) and **Strong-enough-to-publish** (would you put your name on it? show it to a client?). *(Library: P-11 Real Examples Mandatory; P-13 Hormozi Test.)*

---

## The 7 patterns of weak AI content (remove every time)

A diagnostic catalogue — name the failure instead of vaguely sensing it: Filler Opening · Laundry List · Unsupported Claim · Circular Definition · Vague Benefit · Fake Enthusiasm · Dead End. Each has a known fix (show-don't-tell, prioritise-and-describe, end-with-a-next-step). *(Library: F-06 Seven-Step Editorial Process — remove these, then add specifics/proof/offer/objections/CTA.)*

---

## A worked example (the proof niche)

Score the draft cost-transparency page ("how much does the rabies titer test cost in Dubai") against the rubric:

- **True (5/5)** — every number cites a Verified Source Bank row, or is hedged (titer cost C-001: "no official figure"). **Specific (5/5)** — real C-IDs, the IrbisKat quote, the Etihad USD 399 figure. **Trustworthy (5/5)** — the MOCCAE-silence screenshot proves the claim. **Strong-enough-to-publish (5/5)** — you would put your name on it.
- A *competitor's* version of the same page typically scores low here: an **Unsupported Claim** ("we offer competitive pricing") fails Specific and Trustworthy; a **Dead End** ("contact us for a quote") fails Commercial.

That contrast — our 40+/50 vs the market's lower score — is exactly what this skill makes visible and fixable.

---

## How it differs from proofreading

| Proofreading | Editorial Judgment |
|--------------|--------------------|
| Fixes grammar/spelling | Decides if it will rank, convert, build trust |
| Sentence level | Whole-page quality + commercial purpose |
| "Is it correct?" | "Is it true, specific, proven, and publishable?" |
| No score | A /50 score + a publish/hold decision |

---

## Why this is a standalone skill

1. **It is the publish gate** — nothing reaches a customer without clearing it, so it protects the whole content operation from shipping AI mush.
2. **Weak AI content looks the same in every market** — the 7 patterns and 10 criteria are niche-agnostic and reusable.
3. **It is a defensible, teachable rubric** — a /50 score makes "good enough" objective rather than a matter of taste.

---

## In scope / out of scope

**In scope.** Scoring a draft on the 10 criteria, removing the 7 weak patterns, adding what's missing (specifics, proof, offer, objections, stronger CTA), producing the /50 score + edit notes, and the publish/hold decision.

**Out of scope.** Verifying the facts themselves (done upstream — this skill *checks that* facts are cited, not whether the regulator is right), writing the brief that produced the draft, and the page structure. This skill judges and fixes the draft; it does not originate it.

---

## What "good" looks like

- Every published page scores **40+/50** and passes the Content / SEO / Conversion publishing checklist.
- All 7 weak patterns are removed; every generic claim is replaced with a real number, name, or proof.
- No claim survives without a Verified citation (or an honest hedge).
- The edit notes record what changed and why — the score is reproducible, not a gut call.

This skill is complete when the 10-criteria rubric, the 7-pattern catalogue, and the publishing checklist exist for the proof niche, the four universal-gap pages have been run through the full checklist with real scores, and the audit passes.
