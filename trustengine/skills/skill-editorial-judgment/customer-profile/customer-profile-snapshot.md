## Why this matters for editorial judgment

Six of the ten criteria are mechanical (Clear, Commercial, SEO-ish); **four are judgement calls that need the customer in mind**: *Useful, Trustworthy, Brand-aligned, Written-for-the-right-buyer.* You cannot score those without knowing who the reader is, what they fear, and how this brand is meant to sound. This is that context — nothing else from the master profile is needed to score a page.

---

## The reader → "Written for the right buyer" + "Useful"

The customer is **not buying transport — they are buying certainty, compliance, safety, and emotional relief.** They arrive afraid, not curious. Score *Right-buyer* high only if the page opens in their situation and language; score *Useful* high only if it resolves the specific fear they arrived with.

| Persona | Their fear (the page must speak to it) |
|---------|----------------------------------------|
| Import-to-Dubai owner | Confiscation at the airport |
| Expat Leaving Dubai | Documentation mistake at the border |
| Last-Minute Mover | Running out of time |
| Confused Researcher | Overwhelm — doesn't know where to start |

Verbatim reader language (a page that uses it scores higher on Right-buyer): *"without it your dog will be taken away in airport"* (Muze Gu) · *"shamelessly charging an insane amount"* (IrbisKat) · *"every website says something different — I don't know who to trust."*

---

## The brand voice → "Brand-aligned"

**Core emotional promise:** *"Your dog is not cargo. Your dog is family. We manage the process so you know what needs to happen, when, and how your dog gets there safely."*
**What this brand is NOT** (flag if a draft sounds like these): a cargo company · a generic freight service · a budget transport provider.
**Values the voice carries:** Safety · Trust · Reliability · Professionalism · Communication · Expertise. Score *Brand-aligned* high only if the owner would recognise this voice — warm, expert, calm; never hyped or salesy.

---

## What "Specific" looks like here → criterion 2

A page passes *Specific* when it uses the niche's real, checkable data — not generic claims:
- Real numbers/sources: MOCCAE release fee **500 AED/dog** (C-003); permit valid **90 days** (C-010); a real review figure (e.g. **4.8★ / 283 reviews**).
- Fails *Specific*: "competitive pricing", "stress-free", "world-class service", "many happy customers".

---

## What "Trustworthy" requires here → criterion 6

Maximum-risk niche, so the bar is high: every factual claim **cited to a Verified Source Bank row or honestly hedged**; the unpublished is never asserted (titer cost C-001: "no official figure; community 700–1,300 AED"); a real conflict is named, not hidden (Etihad official **USD 399** vs community **USD 1,500**, C-015). No hype, no fake authority, real proof beside claims.

---

## How to use this snapshot

When scoring a draft: read the persona + fear → judge *Useful* and *Right-buyer*; read the brand promise → judge *Brand-aligned*; check every number against the real-data list → judge *Specific*; check every claim is cited or hedged → judge *Trustworthy*. The other six criteria are mechanical and need no profile.
