Editorial Judgment & Quality Control

The quality gate that takes AI content from ~60% to 90%+ before it ships. Not proofreading — this decides whether content will rank, convert, and build trust.

Why this skill exists

AI produces text fast, but most AI content fails: generic, fake-sounding, doesn't convert. Publishing it spends budget on pages that earn no trust and no ranking — in a regulated market, worse than nothing. Editorial Judgment is the gate between a fast first draft and a page you would put your name on.

The reader already feels this. In the Dubai research they said it plainly:

"Every website says something different — I don't know who to trust."  ·  "relocation companies are shamelessly charging an insane amount of money" — IrbisKat (24 upvotes)  ·  "without it your dog will be taken away in airport and never give back" — Muze Gu

Generic, uncited AI content is exactly what makes a reader feel that distrust. The editorial gate exists to make sure the page they land on is specific, proven, and trustworthy instead.

It is not proofreading. Proofreading fixes grammar. This decides rank, conversion, and trust — and produces a /50 score and a publish/hold verdict.

The 10-criteria framework (score /50)

Score each criterion /5 against the page. Publish gate: 40+/50, nothing below 3.

#CriterionScores 5 when…
1Trueevery fact verified or hedged; no hallucination
2Specificreal numbers/names — could not apply to any business
3Usefulanswers the reader's actual question
4Clearscannable; main point obvious in 10 seconds
5Commercialsupports a goal; one clear next step
6Trustworthyproof for every claim; honest not hyped
7Better than competitorsmore specific/credible than the top page
8Brand-alignedthe owner would recognise their voice
9Right buyerspeaks their situation + decision stage
10Strong enough to publishyou would put your name on it
The two that catch the most AI failures: Specific — "we have many happy customers" fails, "4.8 stars from 283 Google reviews" passes — and Strong-enough-to-publish.

The 7 weak patterns — remove every time

PatternExample (real competitor language)Fix
1 Filler Opening"In today's fast-paced world…"open with the reader's problem
2 Laundry List"services include A, B, C and much more"prioritise & describe
3 Unsupported Claim"the leading pet relocation provider in Dubai"show it — 4.8★/283 reviews
4 Circular Definition"quality ensures excellence"delete or make specific
5 Vague Benefit"innovative solutions for your needs"which solution? which result?
6 Fake Enthusiasm"passionate about exceptional results!"show passion via specifics
7 Dead End"contact us for a quote"a help-first next step

The editing process

Five steps: score → remove the 7 patterns → add the strengtheners → re-score → record edit notes.

Add what's missing: specifics, proof, a clear offer, buyer objections, local details, concrete examples, a stronger CTA.

Edit-note example: "removed Pattern 3 unsupported 'best in Dubai'; added 4.8★/283 reviews; CTA changed from 'Get a Quote' to 'Download the MOCCAE checklist'." The notes make the /50 reproducible.

The Hormozi test (criterion 10)

Three questions decide publish:

  1. Would you put your name on it?
  2. Would you show it to a client?
  3. If not — what specifically needs to change?
Higher risk = more proof required. Dubai pet relocation is maximum risk, so an uncited claim caps True at 2 and a could-apply-to-anyone sentence caps Specific at 2 — these are the anti-leniency gates.

Keeping the score honest

The failure mode is a lenient or gamed score. Two defences:

  • Every criterion has a written reason in the edit notes — no bare numbers, no vibe.
  • An independent blind re-score must reach the same publish/hold decision and land within ±4 points; a split = re-examine, never average-and-ship.

The batch audit re-scores 20% blind; ≥90% must agree on the decision. Below that → recalibrate, usually on leniency in True or Specific.
