# Customer Profile Snapshot — Content Architecture
## Only the excerpts an architect needs to shape the sitemap and conversion paths
## Full profile: ../../01-master-customer-profile.md

---

## Why this matters for content architecture

Architecture is shaped by **how real readers move**: which fear they arrive with (their
entry page), what they need next (the spoke they click to), and where that journey ends
(the enquiry). The sitemap exists to carry those journeys in ≤3 clicks with no dead ends.
So the architect needs the personas, their entry fears, and the conversion paths — not the
emotional copy. This snapshot is exactly that.

---

## The readers → entry pages and hubs

Each persona arrives at a different fear, which is a different **entry spoke** under a
different **hub**. The architecture must give each a place and a path.

| Persona | Entry fear (the spoke they land on) | Hub | Click depth |
|---------|-------------------------------------|-----|-------------|
| Import-to-Dubai owner | dog taken at the airport (confiscation) | Import | 2 |
| Last-Minute Mover | can I fly this month? (summer embargo) | Routes | 2 |
| Cost-anxious owner | being overcharged (titer cost) | Costs | 2 |
| Confused Researcher | which airport/airline? (comparison) | Routes | 2 |

The four universal gap pages are the four highest-priority entry spokes — each must sit
at click 2, linked from its hub and back.

---

## The three conversion paths (the journeys the sitemap must carry)

Each path is fear → verified answer → help-first enquiry, and must complete inside the
3-click structure:

1. **Fear path:** fear-resolution spoke (e.g. confiscation) → process guide (what to do) →
   enquiry. *(Import-to-Dubai owner.)*
2. **Cost path:** cost page (titer cost) → comparison (what's included / airport routing) →
   enquiry. *(Cost-anxious owner.)*
3. **Researcher path:** homepage → hub (Routes) → comparison spoke → enquiry. *(Confused
   Researcher / Last-Minute Mover.)*

Every path ends at one enquiry — the single conversion action.

---

## The page inventory drivers → what hubs and spokes must exist

The pages aren't arbitrary; they come from real intents and verified entities:

| Hub | Spokes it must carry | Driver |
|-----|----------------------|--------|
| Import | confiscation, MOCCAE process, documentation | the deepest fears + the regulator process |
| Export | leaving-Dubai process, per-country export rules | the expat-leaving persona |
| Routes | airport comparison, summer embargo, per-country routes (UK, India, AUS, SA…) | location hierarchy |
| Airlines | per-airline rules (Etihad, Emirates, Turkish, Royal Jordanian) | airline rejection fear |
| Costs | titer cost, total-cost breakdown, what's-included | financial-shock fear |

One page per intent across all of them — no two pages chasing the same query.

---

## The brand frame → why structure carries trust

**The promise:** *"Your dog is not cargo. Your dog is family. We manage the process so you
know what needs to happen, when, and how your dog gets there safely."* A buried or orphaned
page breaks that promise — a frightened owner who can't *find* the confiscation answer in
three clicks concludes you don't have it. Structure is part of trust, not separate from it.

---

## How to use this snapshot

Designing the architecture: read the personas → place each entry fear as a click-2 spoke
under the right hub; read the conversion paths → make sure each completes within 3 clicks
to an enquiry; read the inventory drivers → build the hubs and spokes the real intents
require, one page per intent. Everything else from the master profile is unnecessary for
the architecture.
