Content Architecture

The whole site's structure — sitemap, URL rule, navigation, conversion paths — decided before page eleven, so pages don't cannibalise each other or hide where no one finds them.

Why this skill

Build a site one page at a time and the structure happens by accident: three pages competing for the same query, the highest-converting page buried four clicks deep, a dozen pages no link points to, and URLs in four different patterns. Each page can be excellent and the site still underperforms — because nothing decided how the pages relate.

Structure is part of trust. A frightened owner who can't find the confiscation answer in three clicks concludes you don't have it.

"Every website says something different — I don't know who to trust." — the reader who needs your confiscation page to be one click from where they land, not five.
It is the blueprint, not the building. Content Structure designs a page; Internal Linking wires built pages; Content Architecture decides what pages exist and how the site is shaped — the map both others operate inside.

Hubs and spokes

A coherent site is a hub-and-spoke structure: a few topic hubs (pillars), each linking down to its spokes, each spoke linking back up. M-35 Link equity flows down to spokes and back to hubs M-34 — so a page's place in the graph decides the authority it can earn.

The three gates

Structure is proven by measurement, not taste. Three countable gates, every page, no exceptions:

GateRuleCode
1 · 3-clickevery page ≤3 clicks from home (BFS depth)F-33
2 · No orphansevery page has ≥1 inbound internal linkP-37
3 · URL consistency100% conform to the page-type ruleP-36
One page per intent P-23 is the fourth, human gate — two pages quietly chasing one query is cannibalisation a click-count can't catch. Merge them.

The URL rule

Decide the URL structure before placing pages, one pattern per type, so every page conforms from the start. No IDs, no query strings — lowercase, hyphenated, keyword-bearing slugs.

The surprise from the build: a page that "feels" two clicks away can be four — if it's linked only from a sibling, not its hub. Eyeballing the sitemap misses it; only the BFS count catches it. The country-detail pages would have sat at click 4 until the validator flagged them — linking each from its parent route pulled them to 3. Depth is counter-intuitive; the measurement is the arbiter.

Keeping structure honest

Architecture fails quietly — nothing breaks the day a page is buried or orphaned; it just never ranks. Three defences:

  • Re-derive the gates from the sitemap itself, blind to the builder's table — the counts must reproduce.
  • Audit 100%, not a sample — the gates are cheap to count and one bad page fails the whole.
  • The human cannibalisation pass — only a person reading the inventory catches two pages on one intent.

Any gate under 100% (or an orphan, or one URL exception) = restructure, not ship. There is no "95% consistent".
