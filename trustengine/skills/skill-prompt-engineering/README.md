## What This Skill Is

Most people use AI like a search engine.
They type a vague question and hope for a useful answer.
This produces generic, unusable output — every time.

Prompt Engineering treats AI like a team member who needs clear
instructions. The prompt is not a question. It is a creative brief.
The more specific and structured the brief, the better the output.

This skill teaches the 9-element system that turns vague AI requests
into specific, usable first drafts — for any content type, any niche,
any business. The goal is 70%+ usable output without heavy rewriting.

## The 9 Elements

Every strong prompt contains all 9 of these:

**1. CONTEXT**
What is the situation? What background does the AI need?
Example: "You are writing for a luxury villa rental company in Bali
targeting high-net-worth families from Europe and the US."

**2. ROLE**
Who is the AI being? What expertise should it apply?
Example: "You are an experienced SEO copywriter who specialises in
luxury travel content."

**3. OBJECTIVE**
What exactly should be produced? Be specific.
Example: "Write a 600-word landing page for the villa's private chef service."

**4. AUDIENCE**
Who is the reader? What do they care about?
Example: "The reader is a wealthy family planning a Bali vacation.
They value privacy, personalised service, and exceptional dining."

**5. INPUTS**
What information should the AI use?
Example: Facts, features, testimonials, statistics, unique selling points.

**6. CONSTRAINTS**
What should the AI avoid or include?
Example: "Avoid generic travel phrases. Do not mention prices. Keep
tone sophisticated but warm."

**7. EXAMPLES**
Show the AI what good output looks like.
Example: "Here is the style we want: [example paragraph]"

**8. OUTPUT FORMAT**
How should the result be structured?
Example: "Structure as: Headline, 3 sections with H2 headers, CTA.
Use markdown formatting."

**9. QUALITY CRITERIA**
How will the output be judged?
Example: "The page should make a reader feel this is a premium
experience worth enquiring about."

---

## The Minimum Viable Prompt

For quick tasks, use at minimum: Context + Role + Objective + Format.

Example:
"We are a SaaS company selling project management software to
construction firms (context). You are a B2B copywriter (role).
Write a 100-word email to a manager who downloaded our ebook
but has not signed up (objective). Plain text email with subject
line (format)."

## The Revision Loop

First prompt rarely produces perfect output. Plan for 2-3 rounds.

Revision prompt template:
"Good start. Please revise with these changes:
1. [Specific change]
2. [Specific change]
Keep what works: [elements to preserve]"

Key principle: Always specify what to change AND what to keep.
If you only say what is wrong, the AI may fix the problem while
breaking something that was working.

## The 10 Most Common Prompt Mistakes

1. Vague objectives → AI produces generic output
2. No audience definition → AI assumes a generic reader
3. Information overload → AI gets confused, priorities unclear
4. Conflicting constraints → "Be brief" and "be comprehensive"
5. No quality criteria → AI has no standard to aim for
6. One-shot expectations → Planning for 2-3 rounds needed
7. Not saving good prompts → Starting from scratch every time
8. Ignoring output format → Getting walls of text instead of structure
9. Wrong role for the task → Creative writer doing technical docs
10. Not comparing AI models → ChatGPT and Claude produce different results
