## Why this matters for email nurture

A nurture sequence is built from three things and nothing else: **the customer's fears, ranked
by depth** (one per email), **the verified fact that resolves each one** (the body), and **the
help-first offer they actually want** (the CTA). This snapshot is exactly that raw material —
the fear ladder in the customer's own words, the cited facts each email is allowed to use, and
the brand voice every line must match. Write nothing you can't trace to this page or the
Source Bank.

---

## The reader → who is being nurtured

The customer is **not buying transport — they are buying certainty, compliance, safety, and
emotional relief.** They arrive afraid and research-heavy; they *"act quickly once trust is
established."* That sentence is the whole strategy: the sequence's job is not to sell, it is to
**build trust one resolved fear at a time** until the reader is ready to act. The dog is family,
not cargo.

## The fear ladder → one fear per email (ordered by depth)

The sequence walks the fears from deepest (the one that wakes them at 3am) outward to the
practical, then closes on trust. *(Library: M-02 Fear Hierarchy Model — rank fears by
depth/severity; P-05 One Fear Per Email.)*

|-------|--------------------------|-----------------------------------------|
| 1 | Confiscation (Rejection/denial) | *"taken away in airport and never give back… Still when I remember I crying"* — Muze Gu, FB |
| 2 | Documentation mistake | *"every website says something different — I don't know who to trust"* |
| 3 | Time pressure | *"we are moving soon — is there still time?"* |
| 4 | Financial shock | *"being quoted endless amounts"* (7Ssisi) / *"shamelessly charging an insane amount"* (IrbisKat) |

The 9 Column K fear categories these map to: Rejection/denial · Documentation mistake · Airline
rejection · Financial shock · Separation · Wrong provider · Time pressure · Pet suffering ·
Process overwhelm. The sequence covers 7 of the 9.

## The verified facts each email may cite → the body of every email

Maximum-risk niche: every figure is cited to a Verified Source Bank row **or honestly hedged**.
For this buyer, the hedge ("there's no official price; here's the real range — get it in
writing") converts *better* than a confident number, because being overcharged is one of their
named fears.

| Fact | Status | Email(s) |
|------|--------|----------|
| Rabies titer required for entry | Verified (C-019) | 1, 2, 7 |
| MOCCAE held-pet release fee **500 AED/dog** | Verified (C-003) | 1, 4, 7 |
| Import permit valid **90 days** | Verified (C-010) | 2, 3, 6, 7 |
| Etihad pet fee **USD 399 official** vs **~USD 1,500 community** | Verified conflict (C-015) | 4, 5 |
| Titer cost | **No official price** (C-001) — community 700–1,300 AED | 4 (honest hedge) |
| Summer embargo dates | **No single official date** — airline-set | 6 (honest hedge, anchored to C-010) |

Primary source: the MOCCAE export/import pages (moccae.gov.ae).

## The brand voice → every line must be brand-aligned

**Core emotional promise:** *"Your dog is not cargo. Your dog is family. We manage the process
so you know what needs to happen, when, and how your dog gets there safely."*
**What this brand is NOT** (rewrite if an email sounds like these): a cargo company · a generic
freight service · a budget transport provider · a hype-merchant with a fake countdown.
**Voice values:** Safety · Trust · Reliability · Professionalism · Communication · Expertise —
warm, expert, calm. The tone of a knowledgeable friend, not a sales funnel.

---

## What "help-first" means to this buyer → the CTA of every email

This reader has been *"quoted endless amounts"* and distrusts providers. The CTA that converts
gives them the thing that lowers the fear **before** asking for anything: the airport checklist,
the document one-pager, the backward timeline, the itemised cost sheet, the airline map, the
embargo check. *(Library: P-19 80/20 Value-to-Pitch; P-21 One Primary CTA Per Email.)* "Get a
quote" reminds them of being overcharged — it raises the fear it should lower. Offer the help;
the enquiry follows. Only Email 7 makes the booking ask — and even then with no countdown.

## How to use this snapshot

Writing an email: pick the email's fear + its real quote → write the opening (name the fear);
pick the verified fact/hedge from the facts table → write the body (resolve it with a sourced
answer); pick the matching help-first offer → write the one CTA; check every line against the
brand voice. Everything else from the master profile is unnecessary for writing the sequence.
