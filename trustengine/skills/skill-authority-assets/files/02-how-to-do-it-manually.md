# Skill · File 02 — How To Do It Manually
## Documenting one real case start to finish — with proof beside every claim

---

## Why manual first

This skill *is* manual. The asset's whole value is that it documents real work an AI never
saw — so the documenting is human by definition. Automation can later count proof density
(words per citation), but it can never supply the failure that happened on the day. Document
the case by hand; the count just confirms you packed it with enough proof.

Two things on the desk before you start:
1. **The real case** — what actually happened, including what went wrong. If you cannot name
   a real failure or surprise, you do not have a case yet — you have a hypothetical, and a
   hypothetical fails the Hormozi test.
2. **The verified facts** — the C-IDs from the source store that back every claim in the case.

---

## Step 1 — Capture what actually happened (including the failure)

Write the raw account first, plainly: the situation, what was done, what went wrong, what
happened in the end. *(Library: P-15 Deliver Then Document.)* The **failure or surprise** is
the most important sentence in the whole asset — it is the part no AI can fabricate. If the
raw account has no failure, it isn't real enough; go find the real one.

---

## Step 2 — Structure the case (the five beats)

Shape the account into the documented-case structure:

| Beat | What it carries |
|------|-----------------|
| 1 · The fear | the named fear in real customer words (the case opens here) |
| 2 · What happened | the real situation, dated, specific |
| 3 · The proof | the documents/figures that mattered — each cited by C-ID |
| 4 · The failure/surprise | the thing that went wrong — the un-fakeable core |
| 5 · The outcome | what actually resulted (not a hypothetical) |

---

## Step 3 — Cite every claim by C-ID (proof beside the claim)

Beside every factual statement, place its proof: a C-ID from the source store, a named
figure, or a dated fact. *(Library: M-13 Proof Density; F-08 Proof Interstitial.)* No claim
floats. Where a figure has no official source, hedge it honestly (the hedge is itself a
documented fact). The titer cost has no official price — so the case says so, and cites the
*absence*.

---

## Step 4 — Count the proof density (≥1 item per 200 words)

Count the words; count the verifiable proof items (C-ID citations + named figures + dated
facts + the documented failure). The ratio must be **≥1 proof item per 200 words**.
*(Library: M-13.)* Under that, the asset is too much prose and not enough evidence — add real
specifics or cut padding until it clears. Show the count in the output.

---

## Step 5 — Run the Hormozi test

Take a **basic AI prompt** for the same topic (e.g. "write a case study about a pet being
held at Dubai airport") and read what it produces. Then ask: **what is in my documented case
that the AI's version cannot contain?** *(Library: P-13 Hormozi Test.)* The answer must be a
list of real specifics — the named failure, the dated C-IDs, the exact figures, the
counter-intuitive outcome. If the AI's generic version covers everything your case says, your
case is mush — go back and add the un-fakeable real detail. Record the result.

---

## Step 6 — The free-public-work bootstrap (where cases come from)

Before paying clients exist, generate cases by doing **real work in public** and documenting
every step: a free audit, a free relocation walk-through, posted with proof. *(Library: F-14
Free Work Content Bootstrap; F-07 Documentation Loop.)* Each documented piece becomes an
asset and attracts the next case. The loop runs itself.

---

## Worked example — the airport-confiscation case study

**The real case:** a customer importing a dog to Dubai whose titer paperwork was nearly
invalid — caught the day before the flight.
- **Beat 1 (fear):** Muze Gu's *"taken away… never give back"* — the fear, in real words.
- **Beat 2 (what happened):** the titer certificate's validity window was about to lapse.
- **Beat 3 (proof):** titer required for entry (C-019); release fee 500 AED/dog if held
  (C-003); permit valid 90 days (C-010).
- **Beat 4 (failure/surprise):** the *surprise* — the lab's stated turnaround (the figure
  everyone quotes) didn't match reality; the re-test was booked with one day to spare. The
  un-fakeable core.
- **Beat 5 (outcome):** the dog flew; the document was corrected in time.

**Proof density:** four C-ID/figure citations + one documented failure across ~900 words =
≥1 per 200 ✔. **Hormozi test:** a basic AI prompt produces "ensure your paperwork is valid";
it cannot produce the C-019/C-003/C-010 citations, the real turnaround surprise, or the
one-day-to-spare outcome → **passes.**

---

## What you must not do

- **Do not invent the case.** No real failure = a hypothetical = fails the Hormozi test.
- **Do not let a claim float.** Every factual statement carries a C-ID or a real specific, or
  it is cut.
- **Do not pad to length.** Proof density falls when you add prose; add evidence, not words.
- **Do not skip the failure.** The thing that went wrong is the most valuable sentence — it
  is what AI and competitors cannot fake.
- **Do not assert an unverified figure.** Cite it or hedge it honestly (the hedge is a fact).

---

## Output of this manual phase

One documented case study exists with the five beats, a C-ID beside every claim, at least one
documented failure, a proof-density count ≥1 per 200 words, and a recorded Hormozi-test
result. That case study is the real output (`data/case-study-airport-confiscation.md`) and
the input to File 04 — automation counts the proof density; the documenting stays human.
