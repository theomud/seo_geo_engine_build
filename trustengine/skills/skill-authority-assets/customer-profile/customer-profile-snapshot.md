## Why this matters for authority assets

A documented case study is built around **one real person's real situation** — their fear,
what they did, what went wrong, how it ended. So the documenter needs to know whose case
this is, the fear it resolves in their words, the verified facts that back every beat, and
what a *real failure* looks like in this niche (the un-fakeable core). This snapshot is
exactly that — nothing else from the master profile is needed to document the case.

---

## Whose case this is → the subject of the case study

The proof-niche case study documents an **Import-to-Dubai owner** — the persona carrying the
deepest fear in the dataset, confiscation. They are not buying transport; they are buying the
certainty that their dog will not be taken at the airport. The case study is *their* story,
documented.

| The persona | Their fear (the case opens here) |
|-------------|----------------------------------|
| Import-to-Dubai owner | *"taken away in airport and never give back"* — Muze Gu (Facebook) |

## The named fear → Beat 1 of the case

The case opens in the customer's own words, not a paraphrase: Muze Gu's *"without it your dog
will be taken away in airport and never give back from you… Still when I remember I crying."*
That is the real fear the documented case resolves — and using the real quote (not invented
emotion) is part of what makes the asset un-fakeable.

---

## The verified facts → the proof beside every beat

Maximum-risk niche: every factual beat is cited to a verified source by C-ID, or hedged
honestly. These are the citable facts the case study draws on:

## What a "real failure" looks like here → Beat 4 (the un-fakeable core)

The failure is the most valuable sentence in the case — the thing no AI prompt can fabricate.
In this niche, real failures look like: a **titer certificate's validity window nearly
lapsing** before the flight; a **lab's stated turnaround not matching reality**; a document
rejected at the counter for a detail no one mentioned. These are the surprises only someone who
did the work knows — the difference between a documented case and a generic "challenges can
arise."

---

## The brand frame → why the documented case earns trust

**The promise:** *"Your dog is not cargo. Your dog is family. We manage the process so you know
what needs to happen, when, and how your dog gets there safely."* A documented case proves that
promise was kept under pressure — the real titer scare, resolved with one day to spare, is the
asset that earns the link and the citation precisely because it is real.

---

## How to use this snapshot

Documenting the case: read the persona + fear → open Beat 1 in their real words; read the facts
table → cite every factual beat by C-ID (or hedge the titer cost honestly); read the
real-failure examples → make sure Beat 4 documents a genuine surprise, not a generic challenge.
Everything else from the master profile is unnecessary for the case study.
