AI Citation & GEO

Make the page the AI quotes. Answer first, a statistic and a source in every claim, schema the machine can lift, and one entity it can trust. Ranking #1 is worth nothing if the AI cites the page above you.

Why this skill

Half your buyers no longer click ten blue links — they ask ChatGPT or Perplexity, or read Google's AI Overview, and trust the synthesised answer. That answer cites a few sources and drops the rest. If the engine lifts your facts but credits a competitor — or no one — you did the work and lost the visitor before they appeared.

"Every website says something different — I don't know who to trust." — the researcher who now asks an AI instead, and reads whatever it cites.

This skill makes a page citeable, not just rank-able. And it's research-backed: the peer-reviewed GEO study (Aggarwal et al., KDD 2024) found content with statistics + source citations + structured formatting is cited up to 40% more often by generative engines.

Citation, not ranking

Generative engines don't rank ten links — they assemble one answer and attribute it to a handful of sources. M-22 So the unit of victory changes from position to citation.

Indexed (old goal)
Page ranks #1 → the AI summarises its facts → cites the brand on page two instead → your visitor never arrives.
Cited (new goal)
Page leads with a quotable, sourced answer → the AI lifts it and names you → you're the answer, not a link below it.

The 40% trifecta

The KDD 2024 study measured what actually gets cited. Three attributes, together, lift citation up to 40%: statistics + source citations + structured formatting. P-18 This skill puts all three on every page — a named figure, a verified C-ID, and FAQ schema.

The counter-intuitive part: an honest "no official figure" out-cites a confident number. The titer-cost answer leads with "there is no official published price (C-001); the community reports 700–1,300 AED" — an engine prefers a source that discloses what is and isn't official over one asserting a number it can't support. Honesty is a citation advantage, not a weakness.

Direct-answer-first

An engine lifts the opening. So every page answers its target query in the first 100 words, in sentence one, with a statistic and a cited source inside. P-17 Bury the answer and the engine quotes whoever answered first.

Buried
"Relocating a pet is an emotional journey. In this guide we'll explore everything you need to know…" (no answer for 400 words)
Answer-first
"A pet is held at Dubai airport only when its paperwork is incomplete. Release fee 500 AED/dog (C-003); permit valid 90 days (C-010)." (answer + stat + cite, sentence one)

The entity home

An engine won't cite an entity it can't identify. So you designate one canonical Entity Home — a dedicated page, not the scattered homepage P-29 — with one name and description used identically everywhere P-30, tied to verified profiles via a sameAs array F-28, in Organization/LocalBusiness schema. M-23

Fragmented
"PetMove", "Pet Move Dubai", "PMD Relocation" across pages → the engines can't consolidate → no entity → no citation.
One entity home
One exact name + one description + sameAs profiles + LocalBusiness schema → the engines corroborate one trusted entity.

Citation monitoring

Citeability is verified after publication, weekly: run 10 target queries across ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overview and Gemini; record who's cited; act on what you lose. P-48 F-21

weekly: for each of 10 target queries, for each engine:
          record  cited(our page)?  + which source was cited instead
action loop: a query we lose where our answer is stronger
          -> strengthen the answer box + FAQ -> re-submit -> re-check

Record a pre-publication baseline first — for this niche it's generic/uncited (the gap), which is the opportunity the loop then converts into citations.

Keeping it honest

AI-citation work fails in ways that look like success. Four defences catch them:

  • Answer-first — read only the first 100 words; if the query isn't answered there, the engine lifts a competitor.
  • Stat + source in the answer — every figure carries a C-ID or an honest hedge; a floating number won't be trusted to cite.
  • Valid schema, ≥5 Q&A — invalid FAQPage JSON-LD is ignored by the engine; validate it (Rich Results Test, P-47).
  • One consistent entity — the exact same name everywhere, or the entity fragments and the citation goes elsewhere.

A buried answer, a floating figure, invalid schema, or an inconsistently-named entity is a hard fail for that page — and the live citation only counts once the page is published and the monitor confirms it.
