## Why this matters for AI citation

This customer is **Google-heavy before making contact** — and increasingly, "Google-heavy" means
asking ChatGPT, Perplexity, or reading Google's AI Overview rather than clicking ten blue links.
They are *"researching regulations"* in a state of *"confusion → clarity"* need. If the AI's
synthesised answer is generic, or cites a competitor, or cites no one, the most-prepared provider
in the market is invisible at the exact moment of research. This skill makes the page the AI
quotes. To do that you need only three things from the profile: the questions they ask, the
verified facts that answer them, and the entity that should be credited.

---

## The questions they ask → the target queries to win

The profile's search behaviour and the content-gap matrix converge on the same high-value,
AI-answered questions — the ones where a synthesised answer is most likely and most competitors
are silent (9/9 missing). These are the queries the optimised pages target:

| Target query | Page that should be cited | Fear behind it |
|--------------|---------------------------|----------------|
| what happens if my dog is taken at Dubai airport | Confiscation (Fear Resolution) | confiscation |
| how much does the rabies titer test cost in Dubai | Titer cost (Cost Transparency) | price gouging |
| Sharjah vs Dubai vs Abu Dhabi airport for a pet | Which airport (Comparison) | wrong airport / doc mistake |
| can I move my pet to Dubai in summer | Summer embargo (Process Guide) | heat embargo / timeline |

These are *informational/commercial* intents — exactly the queries AI engines answer directly
rather than just listing links, which is why citeability (not just ranking) is the goal.

## The verified facts → what the citeable answer must contain

Maximum-risk niche: every figure in a direct-answer box or FAQ is cited to a verified Source-Bank
row by C-ID, or its absence is hedged honestly. The honesty is also a *citation* advantage — an
AI is more likely to quote a source that names what is and isn't official than one that asserts a
number it can't support.

| Fact | Status | Used in |
|------|--------|---------|
| Import permit applied for online before travel | Verified (C-019) | all 4 answer boxes |
| Import permit valid **90 days** | Verified (C-010) | confiscation, summer |
| Rabies vaccination valid ≥**21 days** after first dose | Verified (C-007) | confiscation |
| Held-pet release fee **500 AED/dog** (250 AED/cat) | Verified (C-003) | confiscation, titer cost |
| flydubai **cargo-only** (no cabin) | Verified (C-022) | which airport, summer |
| Etihad fee **USD 399** official vs **~USD 1,500** community | Verified conflict (C-015) | titer cost |
| Rabies titer price | **No official figure** (C-001); community 700–1,300 AED | titer cost (honest hedge) |

## The entity → who should be credited

The profile positions the brand as a **Dubai-based premium international pet relocation
concierge** — *"your dog is not cargo; your dog is family"* — explicitly **not** a cargo company,
freight forwarder, or budget transport service. That positioning is the entity definition: one
canonical name, one description (used identically everywhere), the real credentials (MOCCAE /
IPATA / trade licence), and the verified social/profile links the `sameAs` array ties together so
the engines corroborate a single, trustworthy entity.

## How to use this snapshot

Optimising a page: pick the target query → write the first-100-words answer that resolves it,
containing a statistic and a cited fact from the table; add 5+ FAQ Q&A from the same facts; link
to the one Entity Home with the exact brand name. Building the entity doc: use the positioning +
credentials above, identical wording everywhere. Everything else from the master profile is
unnecessary for this skill.
