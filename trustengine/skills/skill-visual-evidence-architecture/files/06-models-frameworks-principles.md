## MODELS — the data structures and mental models this skill uses

- **M-37 — Real-Photo Conversion-Lift Model.** The measured case for real process
  photography over stock: swapping a generic image for a real person lifted paid
  signups **+102.5%** in the 37signals/Basecamp Highrise A/B test. This is why the
  brief specifies the real crate, vet check, and handover — never a stock dog.
  *Source:* 37signals (Signal v. Noise), Highrise A/B test.
- **M-13 — Proof Density Model.** Proof sits **beside every claim**, not collected in
  a gallery — one proof-visible visual per claim that needs it. The "proof-visible
  coverage" threshold (≥1 dated screenshot per gap page) is this model made testable.
  *Source:* internal; consistent with Fogg et al. (2003) credibility study.
- **M-19 — Nielsen's 4 Credibility Factors.** Design quality, up-front disclosure,
  comprehensive/current content, connection to the rest of the web — visible,
  dated, official-source evidence delivers all four. Fogg's study found "design look"
  cited in 46.1% of credibility judgements; a real screenshot is the design-quality
  signal that actually carries proof. *Source:* Jakob Nielsen 1999, updated NN/g 2016.

*Also used:* **M-01** Verified Fact (the screenshot makes the verified fact visible).

## FRAMEWORKS — the decision systems and processes

- **F-08 — Proof Interstitial Framework.** The core layout rule: the proof is placed
  **immediately beside the claim it supports**, interrupting doubt at the moment it
  arises — not linked away or buried below the fold. Every screenshot + caption is an
  interstitial. *Source:* internal.
- **F-07 — Documentation Loop.** Component 4 (video documentation) is this loop applied
  to video: complete the real work → capture it with proof → publish it as evidence →
  it attracts the next client → repeat. Capture the real process once, prove it forever.
  *Source:* Hormozi — documentation over creation.
- **F-04 — Source Verification Framework.** The screenshot **is** the verification
  artifact: a dated capture of the live official page is how "link to your sources"
  becomes something the reader can see, not just a hyperlink they won't click.
  *Source:* internal; Google QRG "link to your sources."

*Also used:* **F-31** Who/How/Why (the proof verdict — does this visual genuinely show who/how/why).

## PRINCIPLES — the non-negotiable rules

- **P-06 — Screenshots Are Proof.** Every verified claim needs a **dated** screenshot
  of the official source confirming it existed on that date. The date stamp is the
  whole point — an undated screenshot is an image, not proof. This is why capture stays
  manual (a bot fetch has no human-verifiable date). *Source:* internal.
- **P-39 — Replace Stock Heroes with Real Customer/Team Photos.** Stock "happy
  customer" photos are largely **ignored** — eye-tracking shows readers skip them as
  ad-like. The brief replaces them with the real process or no photo at all. *Source:*
  NN/g eye-tracking via CXL.
- **P-40 — Contextual Relevance Beats Novelty.** A relevant, in-context image (the
  actual MOCCAE page, the actual crate) outperforms a slicker but generic one — proof
  beats polish. *Source:* Sterling Sky 2024 image-swap test.

*Also used:* **P-13** Hormozi Test (real dated proof is un-fakeable — AI/competitors can't replicate it) · **P-07** Independent Verification (the blind re-check of the proof verdict).
